Vector Control Protocol
Ticks - Yard Treatment
Reduce tick populations in the yard through targeted granular application to harborage zones, paired with mosquito service liquid coverage for adult knockdown. Tick control is zone-based - the treatment targets where ticks actually live, not the open lawn where they do not.
APR - OCT
Field Identification
Ticks
Ixodidae spp.
Key marks: Eight legs as nymph and adult (six as larva), no antennae, flattened teardrop body that engorges when fed. Behavior: Quests by climbing vegetation and waiting with front legs extended. Habitat: Shaded humid transition zones - wood lines, leaf litter, tall grass. Does not survive in open sunny mowed turf.
Personnel Scope
Certified Pest Control Technician
Frequency
Monthly During Season (Paired with Mosquito Service)
Materials & Equipment
Granular Spreader, Mosquito Blower, Power Sprayer, PPE
PPE Required
Gloves Safety Glasses Hat Pants Boots Long Sleeves
1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road, if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask the customer where they or their pets have been picking up ticks, and whether the activity is concentrated in a specific area of the yard. Ask whether they have pets that go outdoors, whether the property backs to woods or a field, and whether they have noticed deer, rodents, or other wildlife on the property. Ask about children's play areas and where the family spends the most time outdoors - these become treatment priorities.

2
Inspection and Zone Mapping

Tick control is fundamentally different from a broadcast lawn pest. Ticks do not survive well in open, sunny, mowed turf - they dry out. They concentrate in shaded, humid, protected transition zones. Inspect the property and identify these zones before you treat anything.

Priority Zone Why It Matters
Wood line / property edge The single highest-pressure zone on most properties. Ticks are carried in by deer and rodents and wait in the leaf litter and low brush at the transition from woods to lawn.
Leaf litter and mulch beds Holds moisture and provides the humidity ticks require to survive. Ticks overwinter and stage here.
Tall grass and unmowed areas Ticks climb vegetation and wait with front legs extended (questing) for a host to brush past.
Ornamental plantings and shrub lines Shaded, humid microclimate directly adjacent to where people and pets walk.
Stone walls, wood piles, ground cover Prime rodent harborage. Mice are the primary host for immature ticks and a major driver of yard populations.
Fence lines and shaded perimeters Travel corridors for wildlife and typically unmowed or hard to maintain.
Under decks and shaded structures Cool, humid, and frequently used by pets - a direct transfer point onto animals and into the home.
The Open Lawn Is Not the Target: Blanketing a sunny, well-mowed lawn wastes product and misses the population. Focus your material where ticks actually survive - the shaded, humid transition zones and edges.
3
Granular Application

Bifenthrin granules are the primary tool for tick control in the yard. The granule carries down into leaf litter, mulch, and ground cover where liquid spray does not penetrate effectively, and provides an extended residual in exactly the zones ticks occupy.

🌾 Product Selection

Use either Wisdom Lawn Granules or Wisdom EZ Granules - whichever your branch is carrying for the current season. These are rotated seasonally, so branches will typically have one or the other on hand, not both.

Both carry bifenthrin as the active ingredient and are applied to the same target zones for tick control. Always confirm the current label rate for the target site before application, as rates differ between the two formulations.

Wisdom Lawn Granules (Bifenthrin) - AMVAC or Wisdom EZ Granules (Bifenthrin) - AMVAC
Application Technique

Apply granules to the priority zones identified during your inspection. Concentrate coverage along the wood line and property edges, into and around mulch beds and ornamental plantings, along fence lines and shaded perimeters, around wood piles and stone walls, and to any tall or transitional grass areas.

Apply a band of coverage extending from the wood line into the yard - the transition zone is where ticks move from wildlife habitat into the area the family uses. Do not simply treat the last foot of lawn.

Ensure even distribution with the spreader and avoid heavy overlap or gaps. Sweep or blow any granules off hard surfaces such as driveways, sidewalks, and patios back onto treated areas.

Watering In: Granular applications require moisture to release the active ingredient into the treatment zone. Advise the customer to water the treated areas lightly, or confirm that rainfall is expected. Communicate this clearly - an unwatered granular application will underperform and drive a callback.
4
Liquid Application - Mosquito Service Coverage

Our mosquito service application provides valuable secondary tick coverage. The blower application drives material into the same shaded, low vegetation and foliage where ticks quest, delivering contact knockdown and surface residual that granules alone do not provide on standing vegetation.

When a customer has both mosquito and tick concerns, the services complement each other directly - the granular treats the ground layer and leaf litter, and the liquid treats the vegetation layer above it.

Coverage Focus for Tick Accounts

When running the mosquito application on a tick account, place additional emphasis on the low vegetation along the wood line, shrub lines, ornamental beds, and fence rows. Apply into the base of shrubs and low foliage using the circular/vortexing motion rather than only treating upper foliage.

Refer to the Mosquito Protocol for full application procedure, blower settings, and mix rates.

Pollinator Protection: Avoid drift onto flowering plants and blooming vegetation. Do not treat blooms. Be mindful of wind conditions on both granular and liquid applications.
5
Customer Communication - Lawn Maintenance

Habitat modification is the highest-value thing a customer can do for tick reduction, and it costs them nothing but effort. Ticks require humidity to survive - drying out their habitat directly reduces the population. Walk the customer through these recommendations on every tick service.

Recommendation Why It Works
Mow regularly, keep grass short Short turf dries out and heats up. Ticks cannot survive extended exposure to sun and low humidity.
Remove leaf litter and clear brush Leaf litter is the primary tick habitat and overwintering site. Removing it removes the population's home.
Create a 3-foot barrier at the wood line A dry band of wood chips or gravel between the woods and lawn is difficult for ticks to cross and clearly marks the transition.
Move play equipment away from edges Relocating swing sets and play areas to open, sunny sections of the yard keeps children out of the high-pressure zone.
Stack wood piles neatly and in the sun Wood piles are rodent harborage, and rodents are the primary host for immature ticks. Dry, sunny, organized storage reduces both.
Trim shrubs and let light in Increasing sunlight and airflow reduces the humidity ticks depend on in ornamental beds and shrub lines.
Discourage wildlife access Fencing, removing bird feeders near the home, and eliminating rodent harborage reduces the animals bringing ticks onto the property.
Clear tall grass along fences Fence lines are frequently the least-maintained area of a yard and a direct travel corridor for wildlife carrying ticks.
6
Customer Communication - Personal Repellents

Yard treatment reduces the population on the property, but customers still encounter ticks on hikes, at parks, and on other properties. Personal protection is a meaningful part of the conversation and demonstrates that we are looking out for their family, not just selling a service.

Skin-Applied Repellents

Products containing DEET or picaridin are effective against ticks when applied to exposed skin. Advise customers to follow the product label for reapplication timing and to use age-appropriate formulations for children.

Clothing Treatments

Permethrin-treated clothing is highly effective and is applied to fabric, not skin. It remains effective through multiple washings. Recommended for anyone spending significant time in wooded or brushy areas. Never apply permethrin products directly to skin.

Practical Habits to Recommend

Wear light-colored clothing so ticks are easier to spot. Tuck pants into socks when in brushy or wooded areas. Stay on the center of trails and avoid brushing against vegetation. Shower within two hours of coming indoors - this washes off unattached ticks and is a good prompt to perform a body check. Tumble dry clothing on high heat for 10 minutes to kill any ticks on fabric.

Pets Need Coverage Too: Advise customers to speak with their veterinarian about a tick preventive for any pets that go outdoors. Pets are the most common way ticks are carried from the yard into the home, and yard treatment alone does not protect the animal.
7
Customer Communication - Self Inspection

Tick checks are the last and most important line of defense. Most tick-borne illness transmission requires the tick to remain attached for an extended period, so prompt discovery and removal meaningfully reduces risk. Encourage customers to build this into their routine after any time spent outdoors.

Where to Check

Ticks seek warm, protected areas of the body. Direct customers to check carefully: in and around the hair and scalp, behind and inside the ears, under the arms, around the waist and beltline, behind the knees, between the legs, and in the belly button. Children should be checked by an adult, with particular attention to the scalp, hairline, and ears.

Timing and Routine

Perform a full body check after any time spent in the yard, on trails, or in brushy areas. Use a mirror or have a partner check hard-to-see areas. Showering within two hours of coming indoors makes checks easier and washes off unattached ticks. Also check gear, packs, and pets before bringing them inside.

Proper Tick Removal

If a tick is found attached, advise the customer to use fine-tipped tweezers to grasp the tick as close to the skin surface as possible and pull upward with steady, even pressure. Do not twist, jerk, or crush the tick. After removal, clean the bite area and hands thoroughly.

Advise against folk removal methods such as petroleum jelly, nail polish, or heat. These do not work reliably and can cause the tick to remain attached longer.

Stay in Your Lane on Medical Questions: We are pest professionals, not medical providers. If a customer asks about disease risk, symptoms, or whether they should be concerned about a bite, direct them to their physician. Do not diagnose, do not speculate about what a tick may have transmitted, and do not offer medical guidance. Recommending they contact a doctor is always the correct answer.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"What I focused on today is the wood line and the shaded beds, because that's where ticks actually live - they dry out fast in open sunny lawn, so blanketing the whole yard doesn't do much. Biggest things on your end: keep the grass short, get the leaf litter cleared along that back edge, and think about a wood chip strip between the woods and the yard. And keep doing tick checks after anybody's been out back - behind the knees, waistline, hairline, and definitely check the dog before he comes in."

8
Post Service

After the service is complete, fill out a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting which zones were treated, the products applied, and all recommendations made to the customer.

Knock on the door and report to the customer, emphasizing what you did, what zones you targeted and why, and the lawn maintenance and personal protection recommendations. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Advise the customer that tick reduction is achieved through repeat seasonal service combined with habitat modification. A single treatment reduces the current population but properties bordering woods or fields receive continuous pressure from wildlife carrying new ticks in.

Reinforce that no yard treatment eliminates all risk. Personal protection and tick checks remain essential, particularly for customers whose family spends significant time outdoors or who visit parks and trails.

Document the Zones: Record which specific areas of the property you treated and any conducive conditions you observed - unmaintained wood line, heavy leaf litter, wood piles, wildlife pressure. This supports the conversation on follow-up visits and demonstrates progress when conditions improve.
