General Pest Protocol
Pharaoh Ants
Eliminate Pharaoh ant infestations through a disciplined gel baiting program placed directly on active trails. This species buds and multiplies when treated with repellent or contact insecticides - no sprays, aerosols, or dusts are permitted in active areas. Baiting and patience are the entire strategy.

1
Critical Rule - Do Not Spray

Pharaoh ants are the one ant species where a conventional residual spray will make the problem dramatically worse. Read this before doing anything else on the account.

When a Pharaoh ant colony is stressed by a repellent or contact insecticide, it responds by budding - satellite queens split off with workers and brood and establish multiple new colonies elsewhere in the structure. A single colony becomes many, spread through wall voids across the building, and the account becomes exponentially harder to resolve.

NO residual sprays. NO contact aerosols. NO dusts in active foraging areas. This applies to the interior and to any area where Pharaoh ants are trailing. Baiting is the control method. If you cannot bait, do not treat - escalate to your Service Manager instead.
Warn the Customer Too: Customers frequently spray these ants themselves with store-bought aerosol before calling. Ask directly whether they have been spraying, and instruct them to stop immediately. Recent spraying may already have caused budding and will affect how long control takes.
2
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing ants, in which rooms, and how long it has been occurring. Ask specifically about bathrooms, kitchens, and any area with plumbing. Ask whether they or anyone else has sprayed them, and with what. Ask whether this is a multi-unit building, and whether neighbors have reported the same problem.

Multi-Unit Buildings: Pharaoh ants spread readily between units through shared wall voids and plumbing chases. Treating a single unit in an apartment, condo, or assisted living building will not resolve the infestation. Escalate to your GM to coordinate a building-wide approach.
3
Identification and Biology

Correct identification is essential because the treatment approach is the opposite of most ant services. Confirm before you commit to a plan.

Appearance

Very small, roughly 1/16 inch - noticeably smaller than most household ants. Pale yellow to light amber, nearly translucent, often with a darker abdomen. Two nodes between thorax and abdomen. Requires a hand lens to see clearly.

Colony Structure

Multiple queens per colony and colonies readily split. Nests are diffuse and hidden in wall voids, behind baseboards, in insulation, inside appliances, wall outlets, and beneath flooring. There is no single nest to find and eliminate.

Key Behavior

Requires warmth and moisture, which is why activity concentrates in kitchens, bathrooms, and around plumbing and heating runs.

Trails are faint and often follow wiring, plumbing lines, and structural edges. Ants may trail long distances from nest to food.

Feeds on a wide range of material - greasy, sweet, and protein. Food preference shifts, which matters for bait selection.

A serious concern in healthcare settings. Pharaoh ants are known to carry pathogens and have been documented entering wounds, IV lines, and sterile supplies. Infestations in medical, dental, or assisted living facilities should be escalated immediately.

4
Inspection

The goal of the inspection is to map trails and identify where to place bait, not to locate a single nest. Diffuse nesting means you are following activity, not hunting a colony.

Follow the Trails

Trails are faint and thin. Use a flashlight held at a low angle and follow lines along baseboards, countertop edges, wall and cabinet junctions, plumbing lines, and electrical wiring runs.

Note every trail location and the direction of travel. Bait goes on the trail, not near it.

Priority Areas

Kitchens: Under and behind appliances, inside cabinet voids and hinges, along countertop and backsplash seams, under the sink, and around dishwasher and refrigerator lines.

Bathrooms: Around and behind toilets, under vanities, around tub and shower plumbing penetrations, and at any pipe entry.

Structure: Wall outlets and switch plates, behind baseboards, around window and door frames, in wall voids, and along heating runs and plumbing chases.

Warm equipment: Inside and behind appliances, water heaters, and any warm void.

Do Not Disturb Trails. Wiping down a trail removes the pheromone line the ants use to reach your bait. Leave trails intact and place bait directly on them.
5
Baiting Program

Baiting is the entire control strategy. Foraging workers carry the bait back and feed it to the queens and brood, which is the only way to reach a multi-queen, multi-nest colony.

🎯 Gel Bait Placement

Apply gel bait in small BB-sized placements directly on and immediately adjacent to active trails. Placement accuracy matters far more than volume.

Priority placements: inside cabinet hinges, along the back of cabinet shelving, under sinks, behind and under appliances, around plumbing penetrations, and inside outlet and switch plate voids on the interior face.

Use bait tacos - small folded cardboard or cut glue board plastic - as a clean pallet on finished surfaces.

Use many small placements rather than a few large ones. Diffuse colonies need many feeding points.

Maxforce Fleet Ant Gel (Fipronil) - EnvuVendetta Nitro or Vendetta Plus - MGK
🔄 Bait Rotation and Acceptance

Pharaoh ant food preference shifts over time. If consumption on one bait drops off while activity continues, rotate to the alternate bait rather than adding more of the same.

Check placements at every visit. Consumed bait must be replenished - an empty placement is doing nothing.

Where bait acceptance is poor, verify you are placing directly on active trails and that no repellent product has been applied in the area.

Remove Competing Food Sources. Bait competes with whatever else is available. Advise the customer to clean thoroughly, seal food in hard-sided containers, wipe counters and floors nightly, remove pet food between feedings, and take trash out regularly. Poor sanitation is the most common cause of bait failure on these accounts.
Be Patient. Pharaoh ant control commonly takes 4 to 8 weeks or longer. You may see a temporary increase in visible ants in the first days as workers recruit to the bait. This is the program working, not failing. Tell the customer this before you leave so they do not panic and reach for a spray can.
6
Customer Recommendations

Customer cooperation determines whether this program succeeds. Cover every point below before leaving.

RecommendationWhy It Matters
Do not spray the antsThe single most important instruction. Store-bought sprays cause the colony to bud and multiply, turning one problem into several throughout the structure.
Do not disturb or clean bait placementsWiping away bait or the trails leading to it stops the program. Point out where placements are so they are left alone.
Expect more ants at firstIncreased activity in the first few days means workers are recruiting to the bait. This is normal and expected.
Store food in sealed containersRemoves competing food sources so bait is the most attractive option available.
Clean counters and floors nightlyCrumbs, spills, and grease residue compete directly with bait and extend the timeline.
Remove pet food between feedingsA standing bowl of pet food will outcompete bait every time.
Fix plumbing leaksPharaoh ants need moisture. Eliminating leaks under sinks and around fixtures makes the structure less suitable.
Report neighbor activityIn multi-unit buildings, tell us if neighbors have ants. Treating one unit alone will not solve it.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are Pharaoh ants and they're handled completely differently from any other ant. Whatever you do, please don't spray them - if you spray, the colony splits up and spreads through the walls, and instead of one problem you've got five. What I've done is put out bait right on their trails. Here's the part I need you to expect: for the next few days you're probably going to see MORE ants, because they're recruiting each other to the bait. That's exactly what we want. This takes a few weeks, so stay patient, keep the counters clean so the bait is the best thing available, and leave those placements alone."

7
Post Service and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting trail locations, every bait placement location and product used, whether the customer had sprayed previously, and whether this is a multi-unit structure.

Report to the customer, covering the do-not-spray rule, the expected temporary increase in activity, and the sanitation requirements. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Follow-Up Schedule

Schedule follow-up visits at 2 week intervals until activity clearly declines, then extend as control is achieved.

At each visit: check and replenish every bait placement, reassess trails and add placements where new activity appears, and rotate bait if consumption has dropped while activity continues.

Document activity levels and bait consumption each visit so progress can be tracked and the program adjusted intelligently.

Do not declare control until activity has been absent for several consecutive weeks. Pharaoh ant populations rebound if the program is stopped early.

Escalate Healthcare and Multi-Unit Accounts. Infestations in medical, dental, assisted living, or multi-unit residential settings require a coordinated building-wide program. Notify your GM before beginning treatment so the scope is set correctly from the start.
