General Pest Protocol
Fruit Flies
Eliminate fruit fly infestations by locating and removing the fermenting breeding source, applying drain and surface bio-sanitation, and reducing adult populations with natural catch traps. Source elimination is the service - adult knockdown alone will not resolve the issue.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Fruit Fly
Drosophila melanogaster
Size: ~1/8 in. Color: Tan to pale brown with bright red eyes on the common species. Behavior: Hovers near the breeding material; does not travel far from source. Breeds in: Fermenting fruit and vegetables, spilled sugary liquid, mop water, recycling residue. Cycle: Egg to adult in as little as 7 days.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where the flies are concentrated, what time of day they are worst, and how long it has been occurring. Ask specifically about fruit bowls, potatoes and onions in storage, recycling bins, trash cans, drains, mop buckets, pet bowls, and any recent spills. Ask whether they have had produce sitting out or a bag of potatoes go bad.

2
Identification and Biology

Confirm you are dealing with fruit flies and not drain flies or phorid flies, because the breeding source and the treatment differ. Fruit flies breed in fermenting material, not in the biofilm layer.

Appearance

Approximately 1/8 inch, tan to pale brown, with distinctive bright red eyes on the common species. Slightly stronger fliers than drain flies but they do not travel far from the food source. Hover near the breeding material and are attracted to light.

Key Behavior

Breeds in fermenting fruit and vegetables, spilled sugary liquid, mop water, and decaying organic matter. A single female lays around 500 eggs and the cycle can complete in as little as seven days in warm conditions.

Find the Source or the Job Fails. Fruit flies breed in a specific piece of fermenting material. Spraying adults without locating and eliminating that source produces a brief knockdown and a callback within days. The inspection is the service.
Seven Day Life Cycle: Because the egg-to-adult cycle can complete in about a week, a source left in place will repopulate the space almost immediately. This is why sanitation timelines matter so much on these accounts.
3
Inspection and Source Identification

Work systematically. Fruit flies stay close to their breeding material, so heavy adult activity in one area means the source is within a few feet.

Kitchen Priority Areas

Produce: Fruit bowls, banana hangers, tomatoes on the counter, and bags of potatoes and onions in pantries or under sinks. Rotting potatoes and onions are one of the most commonly missed sources.

Under and behind appliances: Spilled liquid and food debris under the refrigerator, dishwasher, and stove, and in the gap between counter and appliance.

Trash and recycling: Inside the liner and under it, the bin itself, and residual sugary liquid in cans and bottles. Recycling bins are frequently the true source.

Drains: Kitchen sink, disposal, and dishwasher drain lines. Fruit flies will use accumulated food debris in a disposal.

Other: Mop buckets and mop heads, pet food and water bowls, wine and liquor bottles, unsealed juice, coffee makers, and beneath rubber mats.

Bar and Beverage Areas

Where present, inspect bar mats, speed rails, soda gun holsters, ice bin drains, and the underside of bar equipment. These are extremely high risk for fermenting residue.

🔬 Confirming a Suspect Drain

To confirm whether a drain is the source, invert a clear plastic cup lined with a small glue board or sticky tape over the drain opening and return in a few hours. Flies emerging from that drain will be caught on the sticky surface. Use this to prioritize which drains need attention.

4
Source Elimination and Sanitation

Physical removal of the breeding material is the primary control step. Chemical work supports it but does not replace it.

Immediate Removal

Direct the customer to discard all overripe, damaged, and fermenting produce. Remove bags of potatoes and onions that have begun to break down. Bagged waste should go outside to the exterior bin immediately, not to an interior trash can.

Empty, wash, and dry all trash and recycling bins including under the liner. Clean up any spills under and behind appliances.

Clean mop buckets, replace or launder dirty mop heads, and wash pet bowls.

🧺 Drain and Surface Bio-Sanitation

Where drains are contributing, physically break up organic buildup with a drain brush first. Then apply Nibor-D to coat the drain walls, applying around the edges of the drain opening rather than flushing straight down. Foam where equipment is available.

Follow with Bac-Azap as a bio-sanitation treatment to consume the residual organic matter in drains and on surfaces. Bac-Azap can be left with the customer for ongoing use between visits.

Apply DSV to disinfect drain areas and non-food-contact surfaces where organic residue and odor are present.

Nibor-D (Disodium Octaborate Tetrahydrate) - NisusBac-Azap (Microbial Bio-Sanitation) - NisusDSV Commercial Disinfectant - Nisus
Do not apply DSV and Bac-Azap together or back to back. DSV is a disinfectant and will kill the microorganisms in Bac-Azap. Apply DSV first, allow surfaces to dry, then apply Bac-Azap separately.
5
Natural Catch Trap Placement

ClearDefense uses natural catch traps for adult fruit fly reduction. These are non-chemical, food-safe, and appropriate for kitchens and food preparation areas where liquid application is restricted or undesirable.

🍯 Placement

Place traps within a few feet of the identified or suspected breeding source - fruit flies do not travel far, so a trap placed across the room will underperform.

Priority locations: on the counter near the fruit bowl, adjacent to trash and recycling bins, near the sink and disposal, by the pantry entrance, and in bar or beverage areas.

Place traps away from competing food sources and away from air vents and fans, which disrupt the scent plume. Multiple traps are appropriate where activity is heavy or spread across several areas.

Function and Follow-Up

Natural catch traps use an attractant to draw in and capture adult flies. They provide immediate visible reduction in adult numbers, which is what the customer perceives as the problem being solved.

Traps also serve as a monitoring tool - continued high catch counts after the source has supposedly been eliminated indicate a breeding site was missed.

Advise the customer not to move or discard the traps, and note trap locations in FieldRoutes so they can be checked and replaced on follow-up.

Natural Catch Fruit Fly Traps
Traps Reduce Adults, Sanitation Eliminates the Population: Explain to the customer that the traps are handling the flies currently in the air, while removing the fermenting material is what stops new flies from being produced. Both are required.
6
Customer Recommendations

Fruit fly control is heavily dependent on customer participation. Walk them through these points before leaving.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Refrigerate or discard ripe produceRemoves the primary breeding substrate. Do not leave fruit sitting on the counter while an infestation is active.
Check potatoes and onionsA single rotting potato or onion in a pantry bag can sustain an entire infestation and is one of the most frequently missed sources.
Rinse cans and bottles before recyclingResidual sugary liquid in recycling is a major breeding source that customers rarely suspect.
Empty and wash bins regularlyClean under the liner as well - debris and liquid collect at the bottom of the bin itself.
Run the disposal and clean the drainAccumulated food debris in the disposal and drain supports continuous breeding.
Clean spills immediatelySpilled juice, soda, beer, or wine under appliances ferments quickly and is enough to sustain a population.
Dry out mop bucketsStanding mop water is a prime breeding site. Empty and dry buckets after every use.
Do not move the trapsTraps are placed near the source deliberately. Relocating them reduces catch and removes the monitoring value.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"Fruit flies breed in something that's fermenting - and it only takes about a week to go from egg to adult, so as long as that source is here they'll keep coming. I checked the drains and the bins and set traps near where they're worst. Big thing on your end: get any ripe fruit in the fridge, check that bag of potatoes under the sink, and rinse cans before they go in the recycling. The traps will knock the adults down while we starve out the source."

7
Post Service

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting the breeding source identified, areas treated, trap locations and counts, and all sanitation recommendations made.

Knock on the door and report to the customer, emphasizing the source that was found and the sanitation steps required. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations and Follow-Up

Advise the customer that adult flies already present will be reduced by the traps over the next several days, and that new flies will stop appearing once the source is gone.

Because the life cycle runs about a week, a follow-up in 7 to 14 days is appropriate to check trap counts, confirm the source was eliminated, and search again if activity persists.

If trap counts remain high at follow-up, a breeding source was missed. Re-inspect thoroughly rather than simply retreating.
