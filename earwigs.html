General Pest Protocol
Earwigs
Eliminate earwig activity by reducing moisture and harborage conditions, treating exterior breeding sites along the foundation, and performing targeted interior control. Imidacloprid-based products are highly effective. Earwigs are a moisture-driven pest and do not sting or harm people.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Earwig
Forficulidae spp.
Size: 1/2 to 3/4 in, flattened and elongated. Color: Reddish-brown to dark brown. Key mark: Prominent pincers (cerci) at the rear - curved on males, straighter on females. Behavior: Nocturnal, requires damp harborage, hides under mulch, stone and debris by day. Note: Pincers are used for defense and prey; they do not sting and are harmless to people.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they have been seeing earwigs and how long it has been an issue. Ask about irrigation schedules and whether they have noticed drainage problems, standing water, or areas that stay damp. Ask whether the home is on a slab and whether the lot is sloped. Inquire about anything else they would like addressed.

2
Identification and Biology

Earwigs alarm customers because of the pincers. Being able to explain what they are and why they are present resolves most of the concern on the spot.

Appearance

1/2 to 3/4 in, flattened and elongated, reddish-brown to dark brown. Prominent pincers (cerci) at the rear - curved on males, straighter on females. Some have short wing covers but rarely fly.

Behavior

Strictly nocturnal. Requires constant damp harborage and hides under mulch, stone, and debris during the day. Feeds on decaying organic matter and tender plant growth.

They Do Not Sting or Pinch People Meaningfully: The pincers are used for defense and capturing prey. Earwigs do not enter ears, do not sting, and are harmless to people and pets. Reassuring the customer on this is often the most valuable thing you say on the visit.
Earwigs Are a Moisture Problem: An earwig population means there is persistent damp harborage against the structure. Chemical treatment reduces numbers, but the problem returns unless the moisture condition is corrected.
3
Inspection

Earwig calls are typically from customers with slab homes, excessive moisture saturation (over-irrigating or poor drainage), and homes on sloped lots. The slope-facing side is usually the side with the most visible activity - start there.

Pull back mulch or soil from the foundation and note activity. This is the core of the inspection and where you will find the population.

Identify Potential Entry Points

Cracks in the foundation, gaps in the siding starter strip, doors, windows, weep holes, and vents.

Note utility and plumbing line entry points where they penetrate the foundation.

Moisture Conditions to Document

Over-irrigated beds and lawn zones, downspout discharge points at the foundation, poor grading, low spots holding water, and flooding around A/C pads.

Mulch depth against the wall, dense groundcover, leaf litter, and any stone or debris in contact with the foundation.

4
Control - Exterior

Exterior work is where this service is won. The goal is to soak the harborage band along the foundation, not just mist the wall.

💧 Foundation Soak

Pull back mulch and soil - heavy infestations may need a flat shovel - and pin stream soak the foundation using Dominion at 0.28 oz per gallon in the power-sprayer tank.

Also pin stream around porches and patios.

Treat cracks in the foundation, weep holes, siding starter strip, and utility line entry points.

Dominion 2L (Imidacloprid) - CSI @ 0.28 oz/galQualipro (Imidacloprid) - CSI
🌪 Crack and Crevice Dusting

Dust crack and crevice along the foundation, weep holes, and behind the siding starter strip with D-Fense Dust.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Tip for Heavy Infestations: Use a shovel to cut "X" patterns in the soil along the foundation, then use the power-sprayer to pin stream into the "X" patterns. This drives product down into the soil layer where earwigs are harboring rather than letting it run off the surface.
Imidacloprid-based products are highly effective on earwigs. Where activity is heavy, prioritize the Dominion foundation soak over a light broadcast application - depth of penetration matters more than area covered.
5
Control - Interior

Interior treatment handles earwigs that have already made it inside. They cannot sustain a population indoors, so this is targeted rather than broadcast.

💧 Baseboards and Garage

Treat interior baseboards and garage with Suspend PolyZone.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
🌪 Crack and Crevice

Treat cracks and crevices with D-Fense Dust or Doxem NXT aerosol.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSIDoxem NXT - CSI
📌 Monitoring

Place glue boards for activity reduction in high traffic zones. These capture wandering earwigs and give a read on where they are entering.

Glue Boards
6
Communication and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting the affected sides of the structure, moisture conditions observed, entry points found, and all areas treated.

Report to the customer, covering the moisture recommendations. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Keep mulch depth 2 to 3 inchesDeep mulch holds the moisture earwigs require and is their primary harborage against the structure.
Do not overwater lawns and flowerbedsOver-irrigation directly sustains the population. Water less frequently and earlier in the day.
Fix poor drainageAddress flooding around A/C pads and downspouts. Extend downspouts away from the foundation and correct low spots.
Seal gaps and cracksClose foundation cracks, siding starter strip gaps, weep hole gaps, and utility entries where possible.
Pull harborage off the foundationMove stone, debris, dense groundcover, and leaf litter back from the wall so the soil band can dry out.
Follow up in 7 days. Reassess activity, retreat the foundation band where needed, and confirm whether the customer has begun addressing the moisture conditions.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are earwigs - those pincers look nasty but they don't sting and they can't hurt you, and the whole ear thing is just an old myth. What they need is damp ground, and you've got a lot of moisture holding right against that foundation on the low side. I pulled the mulch back and soaked that band, and I dusted the cracks and weep holes. On your end, get that mulch down to a couple inches and back off the irrigation a bit - once that soil dries out they've got nowhere to live."
