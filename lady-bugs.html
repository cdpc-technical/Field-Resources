General Pest Protocol
Lady Bugs
Control and prevention of lady bug overwintering intrusions through heavy exterior treatment of sun-facing walls and upper structure, void dusting at entry points, and exclusion guidance. Treatment is preventative - beetles enter high on the structure and overwinter inside voids, so 100 percent exclusion cannot be guaranteed.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Asian Lady Beetle
Harmonia axyridis
Size: ~1/4 in, rounded and dome-shaped. Color: Pale orange to deep red with a variable number of spots. Key mark: M-shaped or W-shaped marking on the white area directly behind the head. Behavior: Aggregates on warm sun-facing walls in fall; overwinters in voids. Caution: Yellow staining fluid when crushed.

1
Prior to Arrival

Ensure the customer understands that treatment is preventative. It reduces activity substantially but cannot guarantee 100 percent exclusion, because lady bugs enter high on the structure and overwinter inside wall and attic voids that cannot be fully reached.

Set this expectation before the service, not after. Lady bug callbacks are almost always the result of an expectation that was never set correctly on the first visit.

2
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they have been seeing lady bugs, which rooms and which side of the home, and whether it is worse on warm sunny days. Ask whether they have attic access and whether they have noticed activity around windows, light fixtures, or ceiling vents.

3
Identification and Biology

The overwintering species encountered on these calls is the multicolored Asian lady beetle. Understanding the behavior explains everything the customer is seeing.

Appearance

Rounded, dome-shaped beetle roughly 1/4 inch, ranging from pale orange to deep red with a variable number of spots. A distinguishing M-shaped or W-shaped marking is typically visible on the white area directly behind the head.

Key Behavior

Aggregates on warm sun-facing walls in fall and moves into voids to overwinter. Emerges on warm winter and spring days, which is when interior sightings spike. Does not reproduce indoors and does not damage the structure.

Key Fact: Lady bugs do not reproduce indoors. Interior sightings are common in spring and fall. Overwintering sites include shutters, voids behind siding, gaps in trim, and attic and wall voids.
Tell the Customer Not to Crush Them: Lady bugs release a yellowish defensive fluid that stains walls, carpet, drapes, and upholstery, and carries a distinct odor. Advise vacuuming rather than crushing or wiping.
4
Inspection

Lady bug pressure concentrates on the warm side of the structure. Inspect the exterior thoroughly from the ground to the roofline before treating.

Exterior

Identify the warm, sun-facing sides of the structure, typically the west and south exposures. These will carry the heaviest pressure and receive the heaviest treatment.

Look for landscaping touch points where trees and bushes contact brick or siding, providing a route onto the structure.

Inspect for gaps around windows, doors, siding seams, soffits, fascia, rooflines, utility penetrations, attic vents, ridge vents, and behind shutters.

Interior

Look for lady bugs near windows, doors, light fixtures, ceiling vents, and along baseboards. Activity is nearly always heaviest on the same side as the exterior pressure.

Check for staining that indicates the customer has been crushing or wiping them.

Overwintering Sites

Behind shutters, voids behind siding, gaps in trim and fascia, attic voids, and wall voids. These are the areas that drive spring emergence.

5
Exterior Treatment

Exterior work is the entire service. Timing matters - the most effective application is made in early fall before beetles begin aggregating on the walls.

💧 Foundation and Lower Structure

Treat 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground. Pay extra attention to the sun-facing sides where aggregation occurs.

Route or Start Mix (per season)
💧 Mid-Level and Upper Structure

This is where the service is won. Treat around all windows and door frames, siding seams, eaves, soffits, fascia, and exterior light fixtures.

Treat utility and plumbing penetrations, attic vents, ridge vents, gable vents, and behind shutters - these are heavy overwintering entry zones.

Use an extension pole where needed to reach upper trim and soffit lines. Lady bugs enter high, so a ground-level-only application will not control the problem.

Route or Start Mix (per season)
🌪 Void and Crack Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust into crack and crevice at the base of the structure, behind shutters, into gaps in trim and fascia, around utility penetrations, and into accessible wall and attic void openings.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Timing Is Everything: An application made in early fall, ahead of the first cold snap and before beetles gather on the walls, is dramatically more effective than one made after they have already moved into voids. Where possible, schedule these accounts proactively.
6
Interior Treatment

Interior applications are light and targeted. The beetles emerging inside are coming out of voids and will continue to trickle out through spring regardless of interior treatment.

💧 Targeted Application

Place shoe covers before entering. With the B&G on a fine fan spray, treat lightly along baseboards, around window and door frames, and at entry points on the affected sides.

Do not broadcast the interior and do not treat closets unless requested by the customer.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
🌪 Interior Void Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust into cracks and crevices and around utility and plumbing penetrations where beetles are emerging into living space.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Recommend Vacuuming: Vacuuming is the fastest and cleanest way for the customer to handle beetles that emerge indoors. Advise them to dispose of the vacuum contents promptly to avoid odor.
7
Customer Recommendations - Exclusion

Because lady bugs enter high on the structure and overwinter in voids, exclusion is the only permanent solution. Communicate these clearly and document them.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Seal gaps in siding and trimCaulking seams, trim gaps, and fascia joints removes the void entry routes beetles use to get inside.
Screen attic and gable ventsAttic vents are a primary entry point and lead directly into the largest overwintering void in the structure.
Repair window and door sealsReplace damaged weatherstripping, install door sweeps, and repair torn screens.
Seal utility penetrationsGaps around electrical, cable, plumbing, and HVAC line entries are common and easily sealed.
Trim back vegetationRemoving tree and shrub contact with brick and siding eliminates a direct route onto the upper structure.
Vacuum, do not crushPrevents yellow staining on walls, carpet, and furnishings and avoids the associated odor.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are Asian lady beetles and they're looking for somewhere warm to spend the winter. They come in high - up around the soffits, attic vents, and behind the shutters - and then they tuck into the wall voids. On a warm day they wake up and wander into the house, which is what you're seeing. I treated heavy on the sunny sides and up top today. Just vacuum them up rather than crushing, because they'll stain. And if you can get those attic vents screened and the trim gaps sealed, that's what stops it for good."

8
Post Service

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting which sides of the structure were treated, entry points and overwintering sites identified, and all exclusion recommendations made.

Knock on the door and report to the customer, reinforcing that treatment is preventative and that some continued emergence is expected. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Treatments greatly reduce activity but some intrusion should be expected, particularly on warm days through winter and into spring as beetles already inside voids emerge.

Reinforce that this is not a treatment failure - beetles that entered voids before service will continue to work their way out regardless of the application.

Schedule proactive fall service for these accounts. Treating ahead of aggregation is far more effective than reacting after beetles are already in the walls.
