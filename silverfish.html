General Pest Protocol
Silverfish
Reduce silverfish populations through Dekko Pak placement in attics and voids, void and crack-and-crevice treatment, exterior barrier work, and correction of the humidity and starch food sources that sustain them.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Silverfish
Lepisma saccharinum
Size: 1/2 to 3/4 in. Color: Silvery-gray with a metallic sheen from fine scales. Key marks: Carrot-shaped - broad at the head, tapering to the rear. Three tail filaments and two long antennae. Wingless. Behavior: Nocturnal, fast, moves with a side-to-side wriggle like a swimming fish. Requires high humidity. Long-lived - can survive several years, so populations build slowly and persist.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask which rooms they are seeing them in and whether it is day or night. Ask about bathrooms, the attic, the basement and around the water heater. Ask whether they have found damage to books, paper, wallpaper, photographs or stored clothing.

2
Identification and Biology

Silverfish are a moisture and food-source problem. Chemical treatment alone rarely resolves an account - the humidity and the starch supply have to be addressed alongside it.

Appearance

1/2 to 3/4 in, silvery-gray, carrot-shaped with three tail filaments. Fast and wriggling. Often first found in a sink or bathtub, unable to climb back out.

What They Feed On

Starches and polysaccharides: paper, book bindings, glue, wallpaper paste, photographs, cereal, flour, and fabrics such as cotton, linen and rayon.

Conditions They Need

High humidity, typically 75% or above, and darkness. Attics, bathrooms, basements, crawlspaces, wall voids and around plumbing.

Why They Persist

Long-lived and slow to develop. A population can build unnoticed for months inside voids and insulation, which is why a single interior treatment is rarely enough.

3
Inspection

Find the moisture and you find the population. Inspect thoroughly before treating.

Priority Interior Areas

Bathrooms: Under and behind vanities, around tub and shower surrounds, at plumbing penetrations, and in the toe kick.

Attic: A primary harborage. Check along the sheathing, in and under insulation, around the chimney chase, and in any stored cardboard or paper.

Basements, crawlspaces and utility areas: Around the water heater, HVAC unit, floor drains, sill plates and stored boxes.

Living areas: Bookshelves, closets, behind wallpaper, and around window and door casings.

Evidence to Look For

Live insects, cast skins, yellowish stains, small irregular holes or surface grazing on paper, and fine black pepper-like frass.

Moisture Sources to Document

Poor bathroom ventilation, plumbing leaks, roof leaks staining the attic sheathing, inadequate attic ventilation, damp crawlspaces and condensation on cold water lines.

Record these carefully - they drive the recommendations and are what actually resolves the account.

4
Control - Interior

Silverfish live in voids and travel at night. The work is void-directed, not surface spraying.

📦 Dekko Silverfish Paks

Primary tool for attics and inaccessible voids. Place Dekko Paks throughout the attic across the insulation, along the sheathing line, near the chimney chase and around the attic access.

Also place in wall voids via outlet plates where accessible, behind vanities and appliances, in closet corners, and behind bookshelves.

Paks work slowly and continuously, which suits a long-lived pest living where liquid will not reach. Place out of reach of pets and children.

Dekko Silverfish Paks (Boric Acid)
🌪 Void and Crack Dusting

Dust wall voids through outlet and switch plates, into plumbing penetrations under sinks and behind toilets, along attic sill plates, and into cracks along baseboards in affected rooms.

Dust holds in dry voids far longer than liquid and is the better carrier in an attic.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
💧 Targeted Liquid Application

With the B&G on a pin stream or fine fan, treat cracks and crevices along baseboards, under and behind vanities, around tub and shower surrounds, at plumbing penetrations, and along closet and bookshelf edges.

Crack and crevice only. Do not broadcast interior surfaces.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
5
Control - Exterior

Exterior work reduces the population pressing in and treats the harborage feeding attic and wall void infestations.

💧 Power-Spray Barrier

Treat the foundation 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground. Treat around windows, doors, weep holes, utility penetrations, soffits, fascia lines and gable and attic vents.

Route Mix
🌪 Entry Point Dusting

Dust behind the siding starter strip, into weep holes, around attic and gable vents, and into soffit voids where they connect to the attic.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Harborage Reduction

Note leaf-filled gutters, damp mulch against the wall, wood siding contact with soil, and stacked lumber or cardboard in garages and sheds.

6
Customer Recommendations - Moisture and Food Source

Silverfish need damp and starch. Remove either one and the population cannot sustain itself.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Run bathroom exhaust fansThe most common humidity source in the house. Run during and 20 minutes after every shower.
Fix plumbing and roof leaksLeaks under sinks, at water heaters and in the roof deck create the damp voids they live in.
Ventilate or dehumidify the atticAttic humidity sustains the largest hidden populations. Proper ridge and soffit ventilation is the durable fix.
Dehumidify basements and crawlspacesBringing humidity below 50% makes the space uninhabitable for them.
Store paper and fabric in sealed totesCardboard boxes are food, harborage and a breeding site all at once. Plastic totes are not.
Clear old paper, books and cardboardRemoves the food supply directly. Attic and basement storage is usually the reservoir.
Vacuum bookshelves and closetsRemoves eggs and cast skins along with the insects themselves.
Seal cracks and gapsBaseboards, casings, and plumbing penetrations are how they move between voids and living space.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are silverfish. They're after two things - damp and starch, which means paper, book bindings, wallpaper paste, that kind of thing. The ones you're seeing in the bathroom are coming out of the wall voids and most likely down from the attic, which is where they really build up. I've put bait paks all through the attic and dusted the voids, and I've treated under the vanity. The biggest thing on your end is humidity - running that exhaust fan every time and getting the attic ventilated properly will do more than anything I can spray."

7
Post Service and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting where activity was found, moisture readings and conditions observed, Dekko Pak placement locations, all treatment areas, and any damage to books, paper or stored items.

Report to the customer, emphasizing the moisture and food-source connection. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Advise the customer that silverfish do not knock down quickly. Paks and dust work over weeks, and the population is long-lived - expect gradual decline rather than an immediate stop.

Follow up in 14 to 21 days to reassess activity, check and replenish Dekko Paks, and retreat voids where sightings continue.

Where humidity is not corrected, expect recurring activity. Reinforce this at every visit until it is addressed.
