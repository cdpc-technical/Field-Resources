General Pest Protocol
Rat Control
Eliminate rat infestations through thorough inspection, targeted trapping using T-Rex snap traps and the Gauntlet system, exterior rodent station baiting, and exclusion guidance. Rat control requires patience, good notes, and adapting your approach - be smarter than the rat.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Norway & Roof Rat
Rattus norvegicus / Rattus rattus
Norway Rat: Heavy bodied, blunt nose, small ears, tail shorter than head plus body. Burrows in ground; droppings ~3/4 in with blunt ends. Roof Rat: Slender, pointed nose, large ears, tail longer than head plus body. Excellent climber, nests in attics; droppings ~1/2 in with pointed ends.
Personnel Scope
Certified Pest Control Technician
Frequency
Initial + Follow-Up Every 1.5-2 Weeks Until Control
Materials
T-Rex Snap Traps (15-30), Glue Trays, Rodent Stations, Baits, Xcluder, Zip Ties, Attractant, Flashlight

1
Inspection

A thorough inspection is the foundation of every rat job. Inspect the full interior including crawlspace and safely accessible attic spaces. Rats can compress their skeletons to squeeze through openings as small as 1/2 inch and can scale vertical surfaces - inspect high as well as low.

Signs of Activity to Look For

Burrows: Typical of Norway rats - found in earthen banks, under concrete slabs, and under walls. Active burrows will be free of dust and cobwebs with compact, hard soil around the main opening. Rub marks may be visible. Roof rat burrows are less common and shallow, near dense vegetation.

Gnaw marks: Fresh gnawings are rough and light-colored. Older gnawings are smoother and often greasy from the rat's coat.

Rub marks: Dark, greasy smears along runways and vertical surfaces. Fresh marks are soft and easily smeared; older marks are dry and flaky.

Droppings: Norway rat droppings are 3/4" (18-20mm) with blunt ends. Roof rat droppings are 1/2" (12-13mm). Fresh droppings are soft and moist; old droppings are dry and hard. Use a blacklight to locate urine stains in frequented areas.

Tracks and runways: Rats consistently follow the same paths along walls. Active runways are free of dust and cobwebs, have greasy rub marks, fresh tracks, and droppings. Exterior runways may run along fences or overhead lines.

Sounds and odors: In established infestations, listen for scratching, gnawing, and squeaking - especially in walls and ceiling spaces at night. A distinct musky odor indicates a well-established population.

Species Identification

Norway Rat: Prefers meat, fish, and cereals. Burrows in the ground. Found in lower areas - under slabs, in crawlspaces, along foundations.

Roof Rat: Prefers fruits, vegetables, and cereals. Excellent climber - found in attics, along rooflines, overhead utility lines, and in upper areas of structures.

2
Trapping Program

Trapping is a core component of every rat job at ClearDefense. Bring 15-30 T-Rex Rat Snap Traps on every rat service. One of the most common technician mistakes is not bringing enough traps or failing to place them in all areas where rats are active.

🪤 T-Rex Rat Snap Traps - Dummy Setting (Initial Visit)

On your first visit, dummy set all traps - place them with bait but do NOT set the trigger. Rats are neophobic (wary of new objects in their environment). Dummy setting gets them comfortable feeding from the traps before the trap is armed. This overcomes bait shyness and dramatically improves catch rates on your follow-up visit.

Place traps frequently along walls and active runways. Do NOT place them in corners - rats tend to slow down at corners and are more likely to avoid traps there. Place traps up high along pipes and in elevated runways. Use zip ties to secure traps to pipes and other elevated surfaces where they would otherwise fall.

Use food the rats have been actively feeding on as trap bait when possible. Provoke attractant may also be used.

🟡 Glue Trays

Glue trays are NOT the same as standard glue boards. Glue trays have a heavy plastic base, are larger, and have a significantly thicker and stickier glue. Adult rats can easily pull themselves free from a standard glue board but have a much more difficult time escaping a glue tray. Use glue trays as a supplement to snap traps, not as a standalone method.

Heavy-Duty Glue Trays
⚔️ The Gauntlet

The Gauntlet is a CDPC trapping system designed to create a lethal passage rats cannot avoid. It consists of two T-Rex snap traps facing in opposite directions with a glue tray sandwiched between them. Place the Gauntlet in a tight run or along a pipe where rats have no option to go around it.

Initial visit: Set up the Gauntlet WITHOUT the glue tray (dummy set only). Follow-up visit: Add the glue tray and arm the snap traps. Zip ties are essential for securing the Gauntlet in elevated positions.

Trap Placement Rule: Place traps along walls and active runways with the trigger end facing the wall. Rats travel along walls and will naturally run directly into the trigger. Never place traps in open areas away from walls or runways.
3
Rodent Stations and Bait Program

Most rat jobs require a minimum of 4 exterior rodent stations - 5 if the customer has a crawlspace. Place stations near identified entry points, active burrows, and along frequently traveled exterior walls and fence lines. Place interior crawlspace stations along commonly traveled walls.

Bait Selection - Split Station Strategy

On your initial visit, load half your stations with Fastrac and half with Contrac. This tells you which bait the rats in this specific account prefer. Once a preference is established, load all stations with the preferred bait. If consumption of the preferred bait stops, switch baits immediately.

Product Active Ingredient Kill Speed Antidote Pet Risk
Fastrac Blox Bromethalin Fast - lethal after one feeding, death in 2+ days None Higher - no antidote; transfer risk if pet consumes rat
Contrac Blox Bromadiolone (anticoagulant) Slower - multi-feed required Vitamin K1 Lower - antidote available; minimal transfer effect
Pet Households: If the customer has pets, place stations only in secured areas inaccessible to the pets. Be especially cautious with Fastrac on these accounts - consider defaulting to Contrac where pet exposure risk is elevated.
💧 Liqua-Tox II - Last Resort Only

Liqua-Tox II is a water-soluble liquid bait for use on accounts where all other methods have failed. It is best used in dry environments with little competing water. Mix one pouch per quart of water. Liqua-Tox II is a last resort and must only be placed by a GM, Service Manager, or Chief. Do not use without approval.

4
Exclusion

Every possible entry route must be identified and addressed. Rats can squeeze through openings as small as 1/2 inch. Common entry points include utility line penetrations (water, electric, HVAC, drains, vents), holes in exterior walls, foundation cracks, broken windows, warped doors, and unscreened vents.

Xcluder - Temporary Seal

ClearDefense uses Xcluder poly-resin coated stainless steel fill fabric as a temporary seal for gaps and openings. Standard foam exclusion is not appropriate for rats - rat incisors rate 5.5 on the Mohs Hardness Scale (iron is 5.0), meaning rats can and will chew through foam. Xcluder is the correct temporary material as rats cannot chew through the steel fill.

Long-Term Exclusion - Customer Responsibility

Advise the customer that hardware cloth and mortar are the long-term solution for sealing entry points. This is not a service ClearDefense provides. Make it clear to the customer that permanent exclusion is their responsibility and is essential for long-term control. Document this recommendation in FieldRoutes.

5
Sanitation and Habitat Modification

Sanitation is the backbone of a successful rat control program. Norway rats require 0.8-1.0 oz of food and 15-30ml of water per day. Roof rats require 0.5-1.0 oz of food and 15-30ml of water per day. Both species will travel up to 164 feet from their nest to find food. Eliminating food and shelter sources directly reduces the population's ability to sustain itself.

Exterior Recommendations

Remove rubbish piles, debris, and dense vegetation surrounding the structure. Items should be stored at least 18 inches off the ground and 12 inches away from walls or fences. Garbage bins must have tight-fitting lids. Composting areas should never contain human food scraps. Garden produce should be harvested promptly. Pet food left outdoors must be removed after feeding.

Interior Recommendations

Identify and eliminate potential harborage areas - obscured corners, beneath shelves, cabinets, worktables, and out-of-the-way dark areas. Ensure proper storage so inspections can be conducted thoroughly. Reduce clutter that provides nesting material and shelter.

Habitat Modification for Trapping Success

Actively modify the environment to work in your favor. Remove competing food and water sources so bait and trap attractants are more appealing. Move large items to open up runways and place traps directly in those newly opened runs. The goal is to funnel rat movement through your control devices.

6
Follow-Up Schedule and Adaptation

Rat control is not a one-visit job. Establish a clear follow-up timeline with the customer from the start and communicate it in writing.

Service Timeline

Visit 1 (Initial): Full inspection, dummy set all traps and Gauntlets, load rodent stations (split Fastrac/Contrac), Xcluder exclusion at accessible entry points, full sanitation consultation with customer.

Visit 2 (1.5-2 weeks later): Arm all traps, add glue trays to Gauntlets, check and replenish bait stations, document catches and activity, assess bait preference, adjust placement as needed.

Visits 3+ (every 2 weeks): Continue servicing, adapting trap placement and bait based on activity data, until population is clearly declining.

Maintenance (once control is achieved): Monthly visits until full eradication is confirmed.

Adapt Your Approach

Rat treatments are trial and error. If you are not getting trap hits, move the traps. If consumption on a preferred bait stops, switch to the other. If the current approach is not working, try a completely different one. Document every visit thoroughly in FieldRoutes so you and your team can track progress and adapt intelligently.

Document Everything: Take detailed notes on every visit - what you saw, what was caught, which baits were consumed, and any changes you made. Good notes are what allow us to adapt and succeed on difficult accounts.
7
Common Mistakes to Avoid

Rat control is one of the most challenging services we provide. Avoid these common errors:

Not bringing or setting enough traps. Err heavily on the side of more traps, not fewer. Rats are not mice - they are harder to catch and populations are often larger than they appear.
Poor trap placement. Traps placed in open areas, in corners, or away from active runways will not produce results. Place along walls and in tight runs where rats have no choice but to interact with them.
Underestimating the population. Droppings on the ground are often spread throughout a large area by the rats themselves. Concentrate on active signs - fresh droppings, fresh rub marks, active runways - not just the number of droppings seen.
Not making adjustments when traps are not producing. If you are not getting hits after the second visit, something needs to change - placement, bait, trap type, or all three.
Failing to dummy set traps on the initial visit. Skipping the dummy setting step is one of the most common reasons rat trapping programs fail. Never skip it on a first visit.
Not establishing a proper follow-up schedule. Rat control without a committed follow-up schedule will not succeed. Set the expectation clearly with the customer at the first visit.
