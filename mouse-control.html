General Pest Protocol
Mouse Control
Remove active mice from the home using snap traps, identify and seal entry points to prevent re-entry, and place exterior bait stations where appropriate. Unlike rats, mice are curious and neophilic - they respond quickly to properly placed traps.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
House Mouse
Mus musculus
Size: 2.5 to 3.75 in body, slender, with a tail equal to head plus body. Color: Gray with lighter underside; large ears. vs young rat: Mouse has small feet and small head; young rat has large feet and large head. Signs: 1/8 to 1/4 in rod-shaped droppings with pointed ends, grease marks, gnawing, shredded nesting material.

1
Arrival and Customer Interaction

Park in the road if possible or to the side of the home. Clock in to FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask the customer where they have seen mice or signs of activity, when they first noticed the problem, and whether they have heard scratching or movement in walls or ceilings. Ask if they have noticed any chewed food packaging, nesting material, or strong odors in cabinets or drawers. This information will guide your inspection and trap placement.

Replenish Before Every Mouse Job: Always restock your rodent supplies on your assigned office days. Supplementing a lack of snap traps with T1 RTU stations or glue boards inside the home is not acceptable.
2
Identification and Biology

Confirm you are dealing with mice and not young rats before proceeding. Misidentification leads to the wrong control strategy.

Mouse Identification

2.5-3.75" long with a slender body, large ears, and a tail equal in length to the head and body combined. Gray with a lighter underside. Key differentiator from young rats: mice have small feet and a small head. Young rats have noticeably large feet and a large head.

Biology and Habits - Know Your Enemy

Reproduce rapidly: Mature in 35 days with up to 8 litters per year - a small problem becomes a large one quickly. Act with urgency.

Travel range is short: Mice travel only 20-25 feet from their nest. Unlike rats, if you find signs of activity in a specific area, the nest is very close. Concentrate traps in that zone.

Tiny entry points: Mice can fit through a gap the size of a dime (1/4 inch). Exclusion must be extremely thorough.

Neophilic and curious: Unlike rats, mice are naturally curious about new objects in their environment. You do not need to dummy set mouse traps - set them live on the first visit.

3
Inspection

Inspect the full interior with a flashlight before placing any traps. Because mice travel only 20-25 feet from the nest, locating the zone of highest activity is critical to effective trap placement. Inspect the following areas systematically:

Interior Inspection Areas

Kitchen: Inside and behind cabinets, inside drawer spaces, under the stove and refrigerator, inside the toe kick under cabinets, and around all plumbing penetrations under sinks. Look for chewed food packaging, grease marks, and droppings concentrated in specific zones.

Utility areas: Behind water heaters, around HVAC equipment, in laundry rooms, and around any pipe penetrations through walls or floors.

Garage: Along walls, in corners, behind stored items, and around the garage door seal and any penetrations into the living space.

Crawlspace: Along foundation walls, near vents, and at any wood-to-soil contact points. Look for nesting material and active runways.

Attic (if accessible): Near HVAC ducts, insulation disturbance, and any roof penetrations or gaps at the eave line.

Signs of Activity to Document

Droppings: Mouse droppings are 1/8-1/4" long, rod-shaped with pointed ends. Fresh droppings are moist and dark; old droppings are dry, gray, and crumbly. Concentrate of droppings = zone of high activity = where most traps go.

Rub/grease marks: Dark smears along walls and baseboards where mice repeatedly travel. Indicate active runways.

Gnaw marks: On food packaging, wood, plastic pipes, and wiring. Fresh gnawings are light-colored; old gnawings are darker.

Nesting material: Shredded paper, insulation, fabric, or other soft material gathered in a hidden, warm location.

Urine odor: A strong ammonia-like smell in enclosed areas like cabinet interiors or drawers indicates heavy, ongoing activity.

4
Trap Placement - Interior

Interior snap traps are the primary control tool for mice inside the home. Set traps live on the first visit - mice are curious and do not require a dummy-setting period like rats.

🪤 Trapper Mini-Rex Snap Traps - Placement Rules

Place traps perpendicular to the wall with the trigger side facing the wall. Mice run along walls and baseboards - this placement puts the trigger directly in their path. Load with Provoke Mouse Attractant.

Priority placement areas: along active runways and walls, inside cabinet bases and toe kicks, behind the stove and refrigerator, under sinks, inside drawer spaces where droppings are found, and in crawlspaces along foundation walls. Do not place traps in open areas - mice will avoid them.

Do not throw away traps between visits. The scent of previously caught mice on a trap significantly improves future catch rates. Reuse traps unless they are broken.

3-5-7 Trap Density Rule

Scale the number of traps per hotspot based on the level of activity observed during your inspection:

Activity Level Traps per Hotspot Signs Observed
Low 3 Few droppings, unsure runway
Medium 5 Fresh droppings + food access
High 7+ Multiple droppings, live sightings
Glue boards are a supplemental tool only and should never be the primary interior control method. Do not substitute glue boards for snap traps due to a lack of supplies. Restock on your office days.
5
Exterior and Crawlspace Stations

Exterior bait stations intercept mice before they enter the structure and provide population reduction at the source. Station placement rules are strict - follow them precisely.

Protecta EVO Express Station

Use with Contrac Blox. Place along the exterior foundation perimeter. Do not place in backyards where dogs are present. Minimum 2 stations per job.

T1 RTU Station - Restricted Use

NEVER use inside the home. Approved for garages and crawlspaces only. Using T1 RTU stations indoors will produce a foul rodent odor that is extremely difficult to eliminate.

6
Exclusion

Exclusion is essential for long-term mouse control. Mice can enter through any gap 1/4 inch or larger - roughly the size of a dime. Every potential entry point identified during inspection must be addressed. Trapping alone without exclusion will result in continued re-infestation.

Xcluder Fill Fabric

Use Xcluder stainless steel mesh to seal gaps around pipe penetrations, utility entries, gaps in the foundation sill plate, gaps around HVAC lines, and any other opening 1/4 inch or larger. Xcluder is the appropriate temporary seal material - standard steel wool compresses over time and rusts. Cut Xcluder with snips and pack firmly into the gap.

Common exclusion points: Pipe penetrations under sinks, gaps around exterior utility entries, gaps at the base of garage doors, gaps at the sill plate in the crawlspace, and any crack or void in the exterior foundation.

Xcluder Fill Fabric + Snips - Xcluder
Advise the Customer: Long-term exclusion using caulk, hardware cloth, and mortar for larger gaps is the customer's responsibility. Document all entry points found and the recommendation for permanent repair in FieldRoutes.
7
Customer Communication and Follow-Up

Set clear expectations with the customer before leaving. Mouse control typically requires multiple visits and their cooperation is essential.

Advise the customer of the following:

1. Follow-ups are necessary. Schedule the next visit in 3 days. Continue on 3-day intervals until activity clearly begins to decline, then move to weekly until control is achieved.

2. Do not move or throw away traps. The scent of previously caught mice on a trap significantly improves catch rates. Leave traps exactly where they were placed.

3. Maintain sanitation and reduce clutter. Remove competing food sources - store all dry goods in sealed, hard-sided containers. Eliminate cardboard boxes, paper bags, and fabric piles that mice can use for nesting.

4. Store food securely. Pet food left out overnight is one of the most common reasons mouse programs fail. Feed pets what they will eat and remove the bowl.

5. Permanent exclusion is their responsibility. Reinforce that Xcluder is a temporary measure and that gaps must be permanently sealed with hardware cloth, caulk, or mortar for a long-term solution.

Document Everything in FieldRoutes: Record all areas inspected, trap locations, entry points found and sealed, station placements, and all customer recommendations. Good notes allow you and your team to track progress and adjust the program effectively on follow-up visits.
