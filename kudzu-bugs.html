General Pest Protocol
Kudzu Bugs
Reduce kudzu bug aggregation and intrusion through exterior barrier and upper-structure treatment, void dusting at overwintering harborage, and exclusion of siding, soffit and vent gaps. Treatment is preventative and cannot guarantee 100% exclusion.
SEASONAL
Field Identification
Kudzu Bug
Megacopta cribraria
Size: 1/6 to 1/4 in. Color: Olive-green to olive-brown, heavily speckled with dark punctures. Key marks: Squat, oblong and domed - looks like a small beetle but is a true bug. Body widest at the rear, head tucked under. Behavior: Aggregates in large numbers on light-colored sunlit walls in fall. Do not crush - releases a strong odor and a yellow secretion that stains siding and can irritate skin.

1
Prior to Arrival

Set the expectation before the service begins. Treatment greatly reduces kudzu bug pressure but cannot guarantee 100% exclusion - these are strong fliers that land high on the structure and work into gaps in siding, trim and soffits.

Where kudzu, wisteria or soybean fields are nearby, expect heavy repeat pressure during fall aggregation. Say so up front rather than after the second call.

2
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask which walls they are seeing them on and at what time of day. Ask whether they are getting inside or staying on the exterior. Ask whether anyone has crushed one and reacted to it. Ask whether there is kudzu, wisteria or bean field nearby.

3
Identification and Biology

Kudzu bugs are a seasonal exterior pressure pest. They do not feed, breed or damage anything indoors - the entire problem is aggregation on the structure and intrusion into voids.

Appearance

1/6 to 1/4 in, olive-green to brown with dark speckling. Squat and domed with a rectangular outline. Commonly mistaken for a beetle or a small stink bug.

Seasonal Behavior

Fall: Mass aggregation on sunlit, light-colored walls as they seek overwintering harborage. Spring: Emergence from voids as temperatures rise, often appearing indoors at windows.

Host Plants

Kudzu, wisteria and soybean. Proximity to any of these is the single best predictor of how heavy the pressure will be.

Why Customers Call

Sheer numbers on the siding, the odor when crushed, and the yellow staining secretion that marks siding, brick and skin. Some people develop skin irritation or blistering from direct contact.

4
Inspection

Work the exterior first. Kudzu bug service is an exterior job.

Aggregation Surfaces

Check sun-facing walls, typically south and west. Light-colored siding draws them most heavily - white vinyl, light brick and painted trim.

Note where they are densest. That is where the barrier work matters most.

Entry and Harborage Points

Gaps in siding and behind the starter strip, soffit and fascia seams, gable vents, attic vents, window and door frames, shutters, utility penetrations and light fixtures.

Interior Sightings

Windows, light fixtures and ceilings - typically bugs emerging from wall and attic voids rather than walking in through a door.

Adjacent Vegetation

Document kudzu, wisteria or bean fields on or near the property. This drives the expectation conversation and the follow-up schedule.

5
Control - Exterior

The exterior barrier does nearly all of the work. Treat before or at the start of fall aggregation wherever the account history supports it.

💧 Power-Spray Barrier

Treat the foundation 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground.

Mid-level and upper structure is where this pest is won. Treat siding seams, window and door frames, shutters, eaves, soffits, fascia lines and exterior light fixtures - with extra attention to sun-facing walls.

Treat utility penetrations, attic vents and gable ends.

Route Mix
🌪 Void and Gap Dusting

Dust behind the siding starter strip, into soffit and fascia voids, around gable and attic vents, and into gaps around utility penetrations. Use the de-webber head to reach high voids without a ladder where possible.

Do not puncture vent screening.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
🌾 Granular Application

Apply granule around the structure, walking 7 to 9 ft from the foundation, concentrating on mulch beds and groundcover where bugs drop and shelter.

Wisdom EZ or Wisdom G L/N (Bifenthrin) - per seasonal rotation
6
Control - Interior

Interior work is light and targeted. There is no reason to broadcast indoors for a pest that does not breed or feed there.

🧹 Vacuum First

Vacuum live and dead bugs from windowsills, light fixtures and floors. Do not crush or sweep - crushing releases the staining secretion and the odor. Remove the vacuum bag or empty the canister outside immediately.

💧 Targeted Crack and Crevice

Light applications at window and door frames, along baseboards in affected rooms, and where bugs are entering from voids. Do not spray aggregations on interior surfaces.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
🌪 Void Dusting

Dust wall voids via outlet plates and plumbing penetrations in rooms with repeat interior emergence, and attic voids where bugs are overwintering above the ceiling.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
7
Customer Recommendations - Exclusion

Sealing matters as much as chemical treatment on this pest. Bugs that cannot get into the void cannot emerge indoors in spring.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Seal siding and trim gapsRemoves the overwintering harborage entirely rather than treating it repeatedly.
Repair soffit and fascia seamsA primary entry route on two-story walls where bugs land highest.
Screen gable and attic ventsAttic voids are a heavy overwintering zone; screening blocks the route in.
Install door sweeps and window sealsCuts off the secondary path for bugs walking down the wall.
Caulk around utility penetrationsCable, HVAC line sets and electrical conduit gaps are consistent entry points.
Remove kudzu and wisteria where practicalReduces the source population feeding the pressure on the structure.
Vacuum, never crushPrevents staining on siding, brick and interior surfaces, and avoids skin irritation.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are kudzu bugs - they show up on the sunny side of the house every fall looking for somewhere to spend the winter, and light-colored siding pulls them in hardest. Whatever you do, don't crush them. They'll leave a yellow stain on your siding and some people get a rash from it - just vacuum them up. I've treated the whole wall and up into the soffits and dusted the voids they'd be trying to get into. Sealing those siding and soffit gaps is what keeps them from turning up inside come spring."

8
Communication and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting which walls were affected, aggregation density, all treatment areas, entry points identified, and any nearby host vegetation.

Report to the customer, emphasizing the seasonal nature and the value of exclusion. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Advise the customer they may still see bugs on the walls during peak fall movement. Treatment reduces numbers and stops them establishing in voids - it does not create an invisible wall.

Follow up in 7 to 14 days during active aggregation.

Where host plants are nearby, schedule a preventative exterior treatment ahead of fall aggregation each year rather than reacting to the call.
