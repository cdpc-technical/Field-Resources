Vector Control Protocol
Fleas
Eradication of fleas using a spray-out method covering all fabric surfaces and carpet. The paint-the-room technique ensures even coverage without cross-contamination. Success depends on the customer checklist, veterinary treatment of the pets, and daily vacuuming through the pupal hatch window.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Cat Flea
Ctenocephalides felis
Size: 1/12 to 1/6 in. Color: Reddish-brown, flattened side to side, wingless, with large hind legs built for jumping. Behavior: Adults live on the host; eggs fall off into carpet, bedding and pet resting areas. Signs: Flea dirt (black specks that turn rust-red when wet), scratching pets, bites around ankles. Key point: Roughly 95 percent of the population is off-host as eggs, larvae and pupae.

1
Prior to Arrival - Customer Checklist

The customer must complete this checklist before you arrive. A flea job with an incomplete checklist will fail, and the callback is on us. Confirm it before the appointment where possible.

1. Must be out of the home for 90 minutes following treatment.

2. Pets must be on a veterinarian-approved flea treatment.

3. Pet bedding washed in hot soapy water and dried on high heat.

4. Vacuum on the day of service prior to arrival.

5. Pick up clutter from floors.

The pet is the source. If the pets are not on a veterinarian-approved flea treatment, the population will re-establish no matter how well the structure is treated. This is the single most common reason flea jobs fail. Do not soften this point with the customer.
2
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they have been seeing fleas and where the pets spend the most time - sleeping areas, favorite furniture, and which rooms they have access to. Confirm the flea checklist is complete before beginning.

If the checklist is not complete: Discuss with the customer before treating. Vacuuming and washing pet bedding can often be done on the spot. If the pets are not on a vet-approved treatment, advise that results will be limited and note it in FieldRoutes.
3
Biology - Why This Job Works the Way It Does

Understanding the flea life cycle explains the checklist, the follow-up window, and why activity increases before it drops.

Only 5 Percent Are On the Pet

Roughly 95 percent of a flea population is off-host as eggs, larvae and pupae living in carpet, bedding, and pet resting areas. Treating the pet alone leaves the vast majority of the population in the home.

The Pupal Stage Resists Treatment

Pupae are protected inside a cocoon and are not reliably killed by insecticide. They hatch in response to vibration and heat over the following days, which is why activity appears to increase after service and why vacuuming matters so much.

This Is Why Vacuuming Is Mandatory: Vacuum vibration triggers pupae to hatch into the treated environment, where they contact the residual and die. Daily vacuuming for 14 days is not optional advice - it is a core part of the treatment.
4
Inspection

Inspect the entire home for flea activity. Focus on pet resting areas, along baseboards, under furniture, and in carpeted and upholstered zones.

As you inspect, plan your treatment route. Identify which areas are furthest from the exit - that is where you will begin treatment and work back toward the door.

Signs to Look For

Flea dirt: Black pepper-like specks in carpet, bedding and pet resting areas. Turns rust-red when moistened - that is digested blood and confirms fleas.

Live adults: Check pet bedding, carpet along baseboards, and under furniture. White socks worn while walking the carpet will show jumping adults.

Concentration zones: Wherever the pet sleeps and rests will carry the heaviest egg and larval load. These get the most attention.

5
Control - The Painting Method

Imagine painting the carpets and fabric surfaces with a light, even coat. Never walk back over areas you have painted, and always move toward your exit.

This ensures even coverage, no missed zones, and no footprints tracked through treated surfaces.

💧 Using Alpine Flea & Bed Bug

1. Hold the can upright, typically 3 feet above the surface (waist high).

2. Sweep side-to-side with 50 percent overlapping passes.

3. Maintain a steady walking pace.

4. Ensure light, even coverage - not saturation.

Treat: Carpets, rugs, upholstered furniture, pet sleeping areas, and under beds where accessible.

Alpine Flea & Bed Bug (Dinotefuran, Pyriproxyfen, Prallethrin) - BASF
🪛 Hardwood Floors

For rooms with hardwood floors, treat area rugs and upholstered furniture with Alpine Flea.

Use the B&G with Suspend PolyZone on baseboards, and D-Fense NXT aerosol in crack and crevice where debris and pet hair gathers.

Avoid application directly to hardwood floors - it creates a slick, slip hazard.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - EnvuD-Fense NXT (Novaluron, Deltamethrin, Pyriproxyfen) - CSI
Cautions: Aquariums and fish bowls must be covered. Bird cages removed from treatment areas. Avoid open flames and lit pilot lights.
6
Communication and Follow-Up

Set expectations clearly before leaving. Flea accounts generate callbacks almost entirely because the customer was not told what to expect.

1. Allow 2 weeks for complete elimination.

2. Continue heavy vacuuming daily for the next 14 days - this is essential for hatching eggs and pupae.

3. Flea activity may increase the first 7 to 10 days. This is normal and expected as pupae hatch into the treated environment.

4. Keep pets on their veterinarian-approved treatment without interruption.

5. We will follow up in 14 days.

Suggested Customer Talk Track

"Here's the thing about fleas - only about five percent are actually on your pet. The rest are eggs and cocoons down in the carpet. Those cocoons are basically armored, so nothing I spray kills them directly. What happens is your vacuuming shakes them loose and they hatch right into what I just put down, and that's what gets them. So you're going to see MORE fleas for the next week or so - that's the treatment working, not failing. Vacuum every single day for two weeks and keep the dog on his flea preventive, and we'll have this knocked out."

Document in FieldRoutes: Record whether the checklist was completed, whether pets are on a vet-approved treatment, rooms treated, products used, and all customer instructions given. Checklist compliance is the first thing to check if a callback comes in.
