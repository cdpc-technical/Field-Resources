General Pest Protocol
Wasp Control
Eliminate active wasp nests, prevent new nest-building on the structure, and protect customers from stings. Correct identification drives the approach - open comb is a paper wasp, enclosed or underground nests are yellow jackets or hornets. Wasps are a warrantied pest.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Paper Wasp
Polistes spp.
Size: 3/4 to 1 in, slender with a sharply pinched waist and long legs that dangle in flight. Color: Brown to black with yellow or reddish markings. Nest: Umbrella-shaped open comb with visible cells, hanging from a single stalk - no paper envelope. Usually under eaves, soffits, railings and light fixtures. Behavior: Defensive only near the nest; stings repeatedly.
Personnel Scope
Certified Pest Control Technician
Frequency
As Needed - Warrantied Pest
Materials
Liquid & dry insecticides, power sprayer, de-webber pole, bee suit, PPE

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they have been seeing wasps and how long it has been occurring. Ask whether anyone has been stung and whether anyone in the household has a known sting allergy. Reiterate that stinging insects are a warrantied pest and they should never hesitate to contact us. Inquire about anything else they would like addressed.

If anyone reports a sting reaction, direct them to seek medical attention. Wasps sting repeatedly. Do not assess a sting or offer medical guidance - refer them to a physician.
2
Inspection and Identification

Inspect the entire exterior. Search for active nests under eaves, soffits, gutters, behind shutters, light fixtures, and patio furniture. Look for activity around structural entry points such as roofline gaps and chimney caps.

Identify the pest correctly before treating - the nest type determines the approach.

SpeciesNestTreatment Approach
Paper WaspUmbrella-shaped open comb, cells visible, hangs from a single stalk. Under eaves, railings, light fixtures.Direct saturation of the nest - this protocol.
Yellow JacketEnclosed nest in a void or underground. Single small entry hole with steady traffic.Dust the entrance. See the Yellow Jacket Protocol.
HornetLarge football-shaped gray paper nest in trees or upper corners, or concealed in a cavity.Aerosol jet stream or void dust. See the Hornet Protocol.
Open Comb Means Paper Wasp: If you can see the individual cells and there is no paper envelope around the nest, you are dealing with a paper wasp. Enclosed or hidden nests are yellow jackets or hornets and are handled differently.
3
Safety

Work through these before approaching any nest. Every one of them exists because someone got stung.

1. Full PPE - gloves, uniform, eye protection.

2. Bee suit for heavy activity or work in tight areas.

3. Ensure pets and people are inside during knockdown.

4. Determine your escape path before approaching the nest. Know where you are going before you commit.

5. Use slow, controlled movements when positioning equipment.

Take extra precaution on hot midday treatments. Heat increases agitation and the colony is far more responsive. Early morning or dusk is preferable wherever the schedule allows.
Know when to decline. If the nest cannot be reached safely - too high, requiring a ladder position you cannot retreat from, or in a confined space with a single exit - stop and escalate to your Service Manager.
4
Control - Exterior

Approach nests slowly. Use the power-sprayer whenever possible - saturate the nest and spot treat the surrounding area to prevent re-nesting at the same site.

💧 Power-Sprayer - Preferred Method

Saturate the nest thoroughly, then treat the surrounding surface area to leave a residual that discourages rebuilding on the same spot.

With heavy activity, wait 10 to 15 minutes for mortality before approaching further.

Knock the nest down with the de-webber pole and dispose of it - do not leave nests on the ground.

Route or Start Mix (any season)
💧 Aerosol - Where the Sprayer Cannot Reach

Use Stryker Wasp & Hornet aerosol only when the power-sprayer cannot be used, such as light fixtures on a porch or patio.

Stryker Wasp & Hornet (Prallethrin) - CSI
🌪 Entry Points and Voids

For entry points high on the structure, use the power-sprayer but do not flood the area.

For entry voids out of reach, apply D-Fense Dust using the de-webber head.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
5
Control - Interior

Interior work targets the voids wasps are entering through and the fireplace, which is a common intrusion route.

🌪 Void and Fireplace Treatment

Use D-Fense Dust to treat voids, entry points, and fireplaces.

For fireplace inserts, work the dust behind the insert as best as possible.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
DO NOT use aerosols such as Stryker around lit pilot lights. These products are flammable. Confirm pilot lights are out or avoid aerosol entirely in that area.
6
Prevention and Customer Recommendations

Regular exterior maintenance is what keeps nests from re-establishing. Cover these with the customer.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Check for new nests in late springAn early season nest has a single queen and a few cells. Far safer and easier to handle than a mature summer nest.
Keep quarterly service currentThe residual left on eaves, soffits and railings during regular service is what prevents new nest-building.
Seal roofline and structural gapsClosing gaps at the roofline, chimney cap, and soffit removes the void entry points wasps use.
Keep trash sealed and fruit picked upLate season wasps scavenge protein and sugar. Tight lids and clearing fallen fruit reduces foraging pressure.
Check patio furniture and grillsStored and seldom-used items are common nesting sites and a frequent source of surprise stings.
Never attempt DIY removalHomeowners knocking down or spraying a nest is the leading cause of multiple sting incidents.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"That's a paper wasp nest - you can see the open cells, which is how you tell them from yellow jackets. I saturated it and treated the area around it so they don't rebuild in the same spot. I'll knock it down before I leave. Stinging insects are warrantied on your service, so if you spot another nest anywhere on the house, just call us - don't get up there and spray it yourself. That's how people get stung."

7
Communication and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting the nest locations found and treated, species identified, products applied, and whether nests were removed.

Report to the customer and reinforce the warranty. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Follow-Up Schedule

Follow up in 3 days for interior activity and 7 days for exterior activity.

Exterior treatments and maintenance performed during regular quarterly services should prevent future nesting.

Wasps are a warrantied pest - make sure the customer knows not to hesitate to call if the problem persists.
