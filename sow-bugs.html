General Pest Protocol
Sow Bugs & Pill Bugs
Reduce sow bug and pill bug intrusion through granular and liquid treatment of exterior harborage, entry point dusting and sealing, and correction of the moisture and organic debris conditions that sustain them. Both are harmless crustaceans that die quickly indoors.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Sow Bugs & Pill Bugs
Porcellio spp. / Armadillidium vulgare
Size: 1/4 to 5/8 in. Color: Slate gray to brown, sometimes mottled. Key marks: Oval, segmented, armored plates across the back. Seven pairs of legs - these are crustaceans, not insects. Telling them apart: A pill bug rolls into a tight ball and has no visible tail appendages. A sow bug cannot roll, is flatter, and has two short tail-like uropods at the rear. Treatment is identical for both.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are finding them and whether they are alive or dead. Ask about the garage, basement, crawlspace and any door thresholds. Ask whether they have had drainage problems, standing water or heavy irrigation near the house.

2
Identification and Biology

Sow bugs and pill bugs are moisture-dependent crustaceans that breathe through gills. They are an exterior harborage problem that shows up indoors - they cannot survive inside for long.

Appearance

1/4 to 5/8 in, slate gray to brown, oval and armored. Seven pairs of legs. Pill bugs roll into a ball; sow bugs are flatter and cannot.

Why They Die Indoors

They breathe through gill-like structures that require constant moisture. Indoor air dries them out within a day or two, which is why customers usually find them dead or dying near thresholds.

What They Feed On

Decaying organic matter - leaf litter, mulch, rotting wood and decomposing plant material. They do not bite, sting, damage structures, or contaminate food.

Where They Live

Under mulch, leaf litter, stones, landscape timbers, boards, flower pots, and along damp foundation edges. Populations concentrate wherever moisture and organic debris meet the structure.

3
Inspection

This is an exterior inspection. The interior sightings are a symptom, not the source.

Exterior Harborage

Lift and check under mulch, leaf litter, stones, landscape timbers, firewood, boards, flower pots and any debris resting against the foundation.

Check the full foundation line, particularly shaded north and east sides that stay damp.

Entry Points

Door thresholds and garage door seals - the most common route in. Also weep holes, foundation cracks, crawlspace vents and access doors, basement windows and window wells, and utility penetrations at ground level.

Interior Findings

Typically dead or sluggish individuals just inside garage doors, at thresholds, in basements and along crawlspace edges. Live activity inside indicates a genuine moisture problem in that space.

Moisture Sources to Document

Downspouts discharging at the foundation, poor grading, over-irrigation, mulch piled deep and against the wall, clogged gutters, damp crawlspaces and A/C condensate lines.

Record these carefully - they drive the recommendations and are what actually resolves the account.

4
Exterior Treatment

Exterior treatment is where this pest is controlled. Treat the harborage layer, not just the wall.

🌾 Granular Application

Apply granule around the structure, walking 7 to 9 ft from the foundation. Concentrate heavily on mulch beds, groundcover and leaf litter - granule carries down into the damp layer where they actually live, which liquid on the wall will not reach.

Wisdom EZ or Wisdom G L/N (Bifenthrin) - per seasonal rotation
💧 Power-Spray Barrier

Treat the foundation 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground.

Fan spray mulch beds, groundcover and under low shrubs so product penetrates below the surface. Treat around all doors, thresholds, garage door seals, weep holes, window wells, crawlspace vents and access doors, and utility penetrations.

Route Mix
🌪 Entry Point Dusting

Dust foundation cracks, weep holes, behind the siding starter strip, and around crawlspace vents without puncturing vent screens.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
5
Interior Treatment

Interior work is light. A pest that dies indoors within days does not warrant broadcast treatment inside.

💧 Targeted Crack and Crevice

With the B&G on a fine fan, treat along garage and basement wall-floor junctions, at door thresholds, around floor drains and sump pits, and at crawlspace access points.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
🧹 Removal

Vacuum or sweep up dead individuals. Explain to the customer that finding them dead indoors is the expected outcome, not a treatment failure.

6
Customer Recommendations - Moisture and Habitat

Sow bugs are a landscaping and drainage problem before they are a pest problem. The recommendations below resolve accounts that repeat treatment will not.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Pull mulch back from the foundationLeave a 6 to 12 in dry gap at the wall. Removes the harborage right at the entry point.
Keep mulch depth to 2 inches or lessDeep mulch holds moisture and creates the damp layer they require.
Extend downspouts away from the houseWater discharging at the foundation keeps the perimeter permanently damp.
Clear leaf litter and debrisLeaf litter is both food and harborage. Removing it removes the population base.
Correct grading and low spotsWater should move away from the structure, not pool against it.
Reduce irrigation near the foundationDaily watering against the wall sustains the population regardless of treatment.
Move firewood, stone and boards awayStore off the ground and away from the structure. These are prime harborage.
Install door sweeps and garage sealsThresholds are the main entry route. A good seal stops most of the intrusion.
Dry out damp crawlspacesLive activity indoors points to crawlspace moisture that needs ventilation or a vapor barrier.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are sow bugs - they're actually crustaceans, closer to a crab than an insect, and they breathe through gills. That's why the ones you're finding inside are already dead or barely moving - they dry out in a day or so indoors. They can't bite, they don't damage anything, they're just unsettling to find. They're living in that damp mulch along the foundation. I've put granule down through the beds and treated the wall and thresholds. If you can pull the mulch back off the siding a few inches and get that downspout running further out, that dries the whole strip out and they won't have anywhere to live."

7
Post Service and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting where activity was found, moisture and drainage conditions observed, harborage identified, all treatment areas, and entry points.

Report to the customer, emphasizing the moisture and harborage connection. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Advise the customer they may continue to find dead individuals indoors for a week or two after treatment as the perimeter population dies off. Dead bugs are the expected result.

Follow up in 7 to 14 days to reassess, particularly after heavy rain, which drives them toward the structure.

Where drainage and mulch conditions are not corrected, expect recurring pressure. Reinforce this at every visit until it is addressed.
