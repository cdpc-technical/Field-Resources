General Pest Protocol
Stink Bugs
Control and prevention of stink bug overwintering intrusions through heavy exterior treatment of sun-facing walls and upper structure, void dusting at entry points, and exclusion guidance. Treatment is preventative - bugs enter high and overwinter inside voids, so 100 percent exclusion cannot be guaranteed.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Brown Marmorated Stink Bug
Halyomorpha halys
Size: ~5/8 in, shield-shaped body. Color: Mottled brown and gray. Key marks: Alternating light and dark bands on the antennae and along the outer edge of the abdomen - this separates it from native stink bugs. Behavior: Aggregates on warm sun-facing walls in fall and overwinters in voids. Note: Releases a pungent odor when crushed or disturbed - advise vacuuming, not squashing.

1
Prior to Arrival

Ensure the customer understands that treatment is preventative. It greatly reduces activity but cannot guarantee 100 percent exclusion, because stink bugs enter high on the structure and overwinter inside wall and attic voids that cannot be fully reached.

Set this expectation before the service, not after. Overwintering pest callbacks are almost always the result of an expectation that was never set correctly on the first visit.

2
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they have been seeing stink bugs, which rooms and which side of the home, and whether it is worse on warm sunny days. Ask whether they have attic access and whether they have noticed activity around windows, light fixtures, or ceiling vents.

3
Identification and Biology

The species driving these calls is the brown marmorated stink bug, an invasive overwintering pest.

Appearance

Roughly 5/8 in, shield-shaped, mottled brown and gray. The definitive marks are the alternating light and dark bands on the antennae and along the outer edge of the abdomen, which separate it from native stink bugs.

Key Behavior

Aggregates on warm sun-facing walls in fall and moves into voids to overwinter. Emerges on warm winter and spring days, which is when interior sightings spike. Does not reproduce indoors and does not damage the structure.

Key Fact: Stink bugs do not reproduce indoors. Interior sightings are the result of bugs looking to overwinter. Treatment is preventative and cannot guarantee 100 percent exclusion.
Tell the Customer Not to Crush Them: Stink bugs release a strong, persistent odor when crushed or disturbed. Advise vacuuming instead, and disposing of the vacuum contents promptly so the odor does not linger in the machine.
4
Inspection

Stink bug pressure concentrates on the warm side of the structure. Inspect the exterior thoroughly from the ground to the roofline before treating.

Exterior

Identify the warm, sun-facing sides of the structure, typically the west and south exposures. These will carry the heaviest pressure and receive the heaviest treatment.

Look for landscaping touch points where trees and bushes contact brick or siding, providing a route onto the structure.

Inspect for gaps around windows, doors, siding seams, soffits, fascia, rooflines, utility penetrations, attic vents, ridge vents, and behind shutters.

Interior

Look for live stink bugs looking to overwinter, and dead stink bugs near doors, windows, and along baseboards.

Activity is nearly always heaviest on the same side as the exterior pressure.

Overwintering Sites

Shutters, voids behind siding, gaps in trim, and attic and wall voids. These are the areas that drive spring emergence.

5
Control - Exterior

Exterior work is the entire service. Timing matters - the most effective application is made in early fall before bugs begin aggregating on the walls.

💧 Foundation and Lower Structure

Treat 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground. Pay extra attention to the sun-facing sides where aggregation occurs.

General Purpose Mix (Route or Start Mix)
💧 Mid-Level and Upper Structure

This is where the service is won. Treat around all windows and door frames, siding seams, eaves, soffits, fascia, and exterior light fixtures.

Treat utility penetrations and attic vents - these are heavy overwintering zones.

Use an extension pole where needed to reach upper trim and soffit lines. Stink bugs enter high, so a ground-level-only application will not control the problem.

General Purpose Mix (Route or Start Mix)
🌪 Crack and Crevice Dusting

Dust crack and crevice at the base of the structure, behind shutters, into gaps in trim and fascia, and around utility penetrations.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Timing Is Everything: An application made in early fall, ahead of the first cold snap and before bugs gather on the walls, is dramatically more effective than one made after they have already moved into voids. Schedule these accounts proactively where possible.
6
Control - Interior

Interior applications are light and targeted. Bugs emerging inside are coming out of voids and will continue to trickle out through spring regardless of interior treatment.

💧 Targeted Application

Light, targeted applications on baseboards and where stink bugs are entering. Do not broadcast the interior.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
🌪 Crack and Crevice

Treat cracks and crevices and utility and plumbing penetrations.

Doxem NXT - CSID-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Recommend Vacuuming: Vacuuming is the fastest and cleanest way for the customer to handle bugs that emerge indoors, and it avoids the odor released by crushing them.
7
Customer Recommendations - Exclusion

Sealing and exclusion is just as important as chemical treatment. Because stink bugs enter high on the structure and overwinter in voids, exclusion is the only permanent solution.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Seal gaps in siding and trimCaulking seams, trim gaps, and fascia joints removes the void entry routes bugs use to get inside.
Screen attic and gable ventsAttic vents are a primary entry point and lead directly into the largest overwintering void in the structure.
Repair window and door sealsReplace damaged weatherstripping, install door sweeps, and repair torn screens.
Seal utility penetrationsGaps around electrical, cable, plumbing, and HVAC line entries are common and easily sealed.
Trim back vegetationRemoving tree and shrub contact with brick and siding eliminates a direct route onto the upper structure.
Vacuum, do not crushAvoids releasing the strong odor. Dispose of vacuum contents promptly.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are brown marmorated stink bugs and they're looking for somewhere warm to spend the winter. They come in high - around the soffits, attic vents, behind the shutters - and then tuck into the wall voids. On a warm day they wake up and wander into the house, which is what you're seeing. Just vacuum them up rather than squashing, because they'll stink up the place. I treated heavy on the sunny sides and up top today, but the real long-term fix is getting those attic vents screened and the trim gaps sealed."

8
Communication and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting which sides of the structure were treated, entry points and overwintering sites identified, and all exclusion recommendations made.

Report to the customer, reinforcing that treatment is preventative and that some continued emergence is expected. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Treatments greatly reduce activity but some intrusion may still occur during peak movement, particularly on warm days through winter and into spring as bugs already inside voids emerge.

Reinforce that this is not a treatment failure - bugs that entered voids before service will continue to work their way out regardless of the application.

Follow up in 7 to 14 days.

Schedule proactive fall service for these accounts. Treating ahead of aggregation is far more effective than reacting after bugs are already in the walls.
