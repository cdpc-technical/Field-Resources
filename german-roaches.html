General Pest Protocol
German Roaches
Eradicate German Roach infestations from customer homes using a multi-method approach including vacuuming, baiting, dusting, and IGR.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
German Roach
Blattella germanica
Size: 1/2 to 5/8 in. Color: Light brown to tan with two dark parallel stripes lengthwise on the pronotum behind the head. Behavior: Stays indoors, poor flier, prefers warm humid areas near food and water. Signs: Black excrement specks around cabinet hinges and outlet plates, shed skins, egg cases, musty odor in heavy infestations.
Personnel Scope
Certified Pest Control Technician
Frequency
Initial Cleanout + Multiple Follow-Ups
Materials
Liquid insecticides, Sprayers, Bait Gun, Glue Boards, IGRs, HEPA Vacuum, PPE
PPE Required
Gloves Safety Glasses Hat Pants Boots Long Sleeves
1
Prior to Arrival
Customers should be instructed to empty all kitchen cabinets and drawers prior to your arrival. Also to disinfect counters and shelving. Clean out behind appliances should also be recommended.
2
Arrival and Intro
Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory). Ask where they have been seeing roach activity and how long it has been an issue. Explain that their cooperation in following recommendations is vital to the success of the program.
3
Inspection

German Roaches will typically infest the kitchen prior to branching out. For small or new infestations, you may not see live roaches. Inspect every cabinet, drawer, shelve, etc.

Signs to look for: Excrement appears as tiny black dots around cabinet hinges, outlet faceplates, and windowsills. Molting and egg sacs may be on shelving and in drawers.

Large infestations will have a distinct odor. You may see roaches walking on countertops, walls, or dishes in the sink - evidence of a lengthy infestation. No need to inquire as to why.

4
Control

Eradication requires a combination of the following methods:

🧹 Vacuuming

Great tool for immediately reducing numbers, reducing competition with baits, and less breeding. Vacuum up all you see - include moltings and egg sacs (food sources for nymphs).

HEPA Vacuum
💨 Flush Out & Contact Kill

Spray Stryker 54 aerosol into cracks and crevices to flush roaches out for vacuuming. Can also be sprayed directly to roaches for contact kill.

Stryker 54 (Pyrethrin, Piperonyl Butoxide) - CSI
🎯 Baiting Applications

CDPC uses two forms: Gel baiting and dry bait injection (Doxem Precise). Gel baits applied in small BB-sized balls to clean surfaces - cabinet hinges, along shelf lines, inside outlets, etc. Use "bait tacos" (cut plastic from glue board or small cardboard squares) as a clean pallet. Doxem injected into crack and crevice, outlets, appliances, and hard-to-reach areas.

Product Active Ingredient Manufacturer Service Visits
Maxforce FC Magnum Fipronil ENVU Service 1–2
Vendetta Nitro Clothianidin, Pyriproxyfen MGK Service 3–4
Vendetta Plus Abamectin, Pyriproxyfen MGK Service 5–6
Doxem Precise Indoxacarb CSI Crack & Crevice Injection
🌫️ Dusting

Use D-Fense Dust for crack and crevices not being baited - voids under the cabinet, in walls, under and around dishwasher.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
🌀 IGR

Gentrol Point Source can be placed behind appliances, behind separating cabinets, and under sinks.

Gentrol Point Source (Hydroprene) - Zoecon
💧 Spraying

Fill small handheld interior sprayer with Alpine WSG mixed at the high rate (30g per gallon). Spray baseboards, behind appliances, under dishwasher, and suspected avenues - not on food prep areas, food storage, dishes, or dish storage.

Alpine WSG 0.30% (Dinotefuran) - BASF @ 30g/gal
📌 Glue Board Placement

Place glue traps on every shelf, cabinet, behind small countertop appliances, under sink, behind fridge and stove. Provides both immediate number reduction and data on concentrated nesting locations.

Catchmaster CDPC Glue Boards
5
Communication & Follow-Up

Explain the following to your customer:

1. Wait at least 30 minutes prior to placing things back in cabinets to allow product to dry.
2. Keep the area clean and spill free - no competition with baits. Clean up excrement if found.
3. Don't move or throw away glue traps.
4. Don't use DIY sprays.
5. The office will contact them to set a follow-up for two weeks out. Explain that elimination of German Roaches will take more than one treatment - especially for larger infestations.
