General Pest Protocol
Black Widow Spiders
Eliminate black widow populations through thorough mechanical de-webbing and egg sac removal, liberal void dusting of harborage areas, and targeted liquid treatment of garages, foundations, and interior entry points. Harborage reduction and customer safety guidance are essential components of this service.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they have seen the spiders or webbing and how long it has been an issue. Ask specifically about the garage, crawlspace, under decks and steps, around meter boxes and hose reels, and in stored items. Ask whether there are children or pets who play in those areas, and whether anyone has been bitten.

If the customer reports a suspected bite, direct them to seek medical attention. Do not assess the bite, do not identify the spider from a wound, and do not offer medical guidance. We are pest professionals, not medical providers. Recommending they contact a physician is always the correct answer.
2
Identification and Biology

Correct identification matters here because black widow is a medically significant species and the customer will want confidence in what you tell them.

Adult Female - The Concern

Glossy jet black, roughly 1/2 inch body with long legs. The distinctive red hourglass marking on the underside of the abdomen is the definitive identifier. Hangs upside down in the web, which makes the marking visible. Females are the medically significant sex.

Male and Juveniles

Males are substantially smaller, lighter in color, and are not considered a medical concern. Juveniles are patterned with white, brown, and orange markings and darken as they mature. Egg sacs are round, papery, and tan to off-white, roughly 1/2 inch.

Web Structure Is a Giveaway: Black widows build irregular, tangled, three-dimensional webs with no organized pattern. The silk is noticeably strong and coarse compared to other spiders. Webs are built low and in protected voids and corners.
Behavior to Explain to the Customer

Black widows are reclusive and non-aggressive. They do not hunt or pursue people and bites almost always occur when the spider is pressed against skin - reaching into stored boxes, putting on shoes or gloves left in a garage, or moving material where a web is built.

They prefer dark, undisturbed, protected voids close to the ground. Activity increases through the warm months.

3
Inspection

Black widows occupy predictable locations. Use a flashlight and work low and into protected voids. Wear gloves throughout the inspection.

Exterior Priority Areas

Ground-level voids: Under decks, steps, and porches. Inside and behind stacked stone, block, brick, and landscape timbers. Under and behind AC condensers.

Utility and fixtures: Meter boxes, electrical boxes, irrigation valve boxes, hose reels and hose bibs, and exterior light fixtures.

Stored material: Firewood piles, lumber, stored pipe, children's play equipment, trash and recycling bin undersides, and any long-undisturbed item.

Structure: Foundation cracks and weep holes, crawlspace vents and access doors, window wells, and the underside of eaves and overhangs at ground level.

Interior and Outbuilding Areas

Garage: Corners at floor level, behind and under stored boxes and shelving, along the garage door track and under the seal, behind the water heater, and in the void behind hanging items. Garages are the single most common location on residential accounts.

Crawlspace: Along sill plates, around piers, in corners, and near the access door.

Basement and sheds: Floor-level corners, behind stored items, under workbenches, and in undisturbed clutter.

Note Every Egg Sac: A single egg sac contains a large number of spiderlings. Document and physically remove every egg sac you find - this is one of the highest-impact actions on the service.
4
Mechanical Removal and De-Webbing

Physical removal comes first. Mechanical de-webbing removes the spiders, their webs, and their egg sacs immediately and makes the follow-up treatment far more effective.

🧹 De-Webbing Procedure

Dust the de-web head with D-Fense Dust prior to de-webbing (do this in a grassy area and be careful of drift), then de-web the full structure using the Cotton Candy Method.

De-web all corners, doors, windows, soffits, below siding, downspouts, above and below decks, around lights, and around the mailbox. De-web the garage at baseboards, ceiling, windows, and garage door, paying close attention to the area above the garage door when open. De-web the interior of the crawlspace door.

Physically remove all egg sacs encountered and dispose of them off site or in a sealed bag. Do not simply knock them to the ground.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Wear gloves at all times during de-webbing and when reaching into voids. Never reach blindly into a void, box, or stored material. Use a flashlight and look before you reach.
5
Chemical Treatment

Treat the harborage voids and the surfaces spiders travel across. Black widows spend nearly all of their time in protected voids, which is why dust does the heavy lifting on this service.

🌪 Void and Harborage Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust liberally into all identified harborage voids: under decks and steps, into stacked stone and block voids, behind and under AC units, into meter and utility boxes, around hose bibs, into foundation cracks and weep holes, and along crawlspace sill plates.

In the garage, dust floor-level corners, the void behind and under shelving, along the garage door track, behind the water heater, and any wall void openings.

Dust is the primary tool here. Be thorough and be liberal in voids where spiders are harboring.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
💧 Liquid Application - Garage and Interior

With the B&G on a fine fan spray, treat the garage along all baseboards, 6 inches up and 6 inches out. Treat behind storage, up the garage door slides, and other junctions where spiders and webs are commonly found. Treat below all sinks where pipes come through walls.

Where interior activity is present, treat lightly along baseboards in affected areas, in floor-level corners, and around plumbing penetrations.

Onslaught FastCap (Multi Active) - MGK
💧 Exterior Power-Spray

Treat the foundation 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground. Treat around all doors, windows, weep holes, utility penetrations, crawlspace vents, and the siding starter strip.

Treat under decks and steps, around porch skirting, and into landscape and stone areas where harborage was identified.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envuor Bifen IT (Bifenthrin) - CSI
📌 Glue Board Monitoring

Place glue boards in garages, crawlspaces, and basements at floor level in corners and along walls where activity was found. These capture wandering spiders and provide a read on population levels for the follow-up visit.

Place glue boards out of reach of pets and children.

Trapper Max Glue Boards - Bell Labs
6
Customer Recommendations - Harborage and Safety

Harborage reduction is what keeps black widows from re-establishing, and the safety guidance genuinely protects the family. Cover both.

RecommendationWhy It Matters
Wear gloves in the garage and yardNearly all bites happen when a spider is pressed against skin. Gloves when moving boxes, firewood, or stored material eliminate most of the risk.
Shake out shoes, gloves, and clothingItems stored in garages, sheds, or crawlspaces should be shaken out before use.
Store items in sealed plastic binsCardboard boxes on garage floors are ideal harborage. Sealed plastic totes off the floor remove it.
Declutter and elevate storageKeep stored items off the floor and away from walls so voids and corners stay accessible and visible.
Move firewood away from the homeStore firewood off the ground and well away from the structure. Wear gloves when handling it.
Clear exterior harborageRemove stacked stone, debris piles, unused pots, and lumber from against the foundation and around outbuildings.
Reduce exterior lighting or switch bulbsLights attract the insects black widows feed on. Yellow bulbs or relocating fixtures reduces the prey supply.
Seal entry pointsInstall door sweeps and garage door seals, caulk foundation cracks, and repair crawlspace vent screens.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"Black widows are reclusive - they're not going to come after anybody. Almost every bite happens when someone reaches into a box or puts on a glove that's been sitting in the garage. So the number one thing is gloves, and shake things out before you use them. I de-webbed everything, pulled the egg sacs I found, and dusted all the voids where they hide. Getting those cardboard boxes up off the garage floor and into sealed totes would take away most of what's left."

7
Post Service

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting where spiders and egg sacs were found, how many egg sacs were removed, all areas treated, and glue board locations.

Knock on the door and report to the customer, walking through what was found and removed and covering the safety recommendations. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations and Follow-Up

Advise the customer they may see spiders for a period after treatment as they contact the residual and are flushed from voids. Seeing dead spiders is the treatment working.

Because egg sacs can hatch after service, a follow-up visit is appropriate to check glue boards, remove any new egg sacs, and retreat harborage areas.

Advise the customer to contact us if they find new egg sacs or continued live activity, and to avoid disturbing treated voids.
