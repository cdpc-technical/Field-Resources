General Pest Protocol
House Centipedes
Eliminate house centipede activity by treating harborage voids and moisture areas, controlling the prey insects sustaining them, and correcting the humidity conditions they require. Centipedes are a predatory nuisance pest - they are a symptom of moisture and available prey.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
House Centipede
Scutigera coleoptrata
Size: 1 to 1.5 in body; appears much larger due to legs. Color: Grayish-yellow with three dark stripes down the back. Key mark: 15 pairs of very long delicate legs, one pair per segment. Extremely fast. Behavior: Nocturnal predator of other insects; requires high humidity.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing centipedes, whether it is mostly at night, and how long it has been an issue. Ask specifically about bathrooms, basements, crawlspaces, and laundry areas. Ask whether they have had any plumbing leaks, drainage issues, or standing water. Ask what other insects they have been seeing, since centipedes follow their prey.

2
Identification and Biology

House centipedes alarm customers more than almost any other occasional invader. Being able to explain what they are and why they are present builds immediate confidence.

Appearance

Roughly 1 to 1.5 inches long with a flattened, grayish-yellow body marked by three dark stripes. Fifteen pairs of very long, delicate legs give it a much larger apparent size. Extremely fast moving. The rear legs on females are longer than the body.

Key Behavior

Nocturnal predator. Feeds on roaches, silverfish, spiders, flies, and other soft-bodied insects. Requires high humidity to survive and dies quickly in dry conditions. Does not damage the structure, food, or fabrics.

Centipedes Are a Symptom, Not the Cause: A house centipede population means there is a steady supply of prey insects and enough moisture to sustain them. Controlling the underlying insect population and drying out harborage areas is what solves the problem long term.
Customer Question You Will Get: House centipedes can bite if handled, but bites are rare and are generally comparable to a bee sting. They are not aggressive and are not considered a medical threat. Do not speculate beyond this - if a customer reports a reaction, direct them to their physician.
3
Inspection

Inspect for both moisture and prey. Centipedes concentrate wherever humidity and insect activity overlap.

Interior Priority Areas

Bathrooms: Around and behind toilets, under vanities, around tub and shower plumbing penetrations, and floor drains.

Basements and crawlspaces: Along sill plates, around floor drains, behind stored boxes, and in any area with dampness or condensation.

Laundry and utility: Behind washers and dryers, around water heater pans, and at all plumbing penetrations.

Kitchens: Under sinks, behind the dishwasher, and around plumbing entries.

Look for prey evidence at the same time - roach droppings, silverfish damage, spider webbing, and accumulated dead insects in window wells and light fixtures.

Exterior and Moisture Sources

Check leaf litter, mulch beds, groundcover, stacked firewood, stone, and landscape timbers against the foundation. These are prime exterior harborage.

Identify moisture problems: leaking spigots, downspouts discharging at the foundation, poor grading, clogged gutters, over-irrigation, and crawlspace humidity or standing water.

4
Interior Treatment

Interior work targets the harborage areas and the voids centipedes travel through, not open floor space.

💧 Liquid Application

With the B&G on a fine fan spray, treat along baseboards in affected rooms, behind and under appliances, under sinks and vanities, around tub and shower plumbing, and behind toilets.

Concentrate on bathrooms, laundry areas, basements, and any room where activity was reported.

Onslaught FastCap (Multi Active) - MGK
🌪 Void and Crack Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust into wall voids via plumbing penetrations, behind escutcheon plates, into the void under vanities and cabinet toe kicks, around floor drains, and along sill plates in basements and crawlspaces.

Dust is critical here because centipedes spend the majority of their time in voids and only emerge at night.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Treat the Prey Too: If the inspection turned up roaches, silverfish, or spiders, treat for those pests as part of the same service. Removing the food source is the most reliable way to eliminate a centipede population.
5
Exterior Treatment

Exterior treatment reduces the population pressing in from harborage around the structure and intercepts centipedes at entry points.

💧 Power-Spray Barrier

Treat the foundation 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground. Treat heavily into mulch beds, groundcover, and landscape areas where product should reach below the surface.

Spray around all doors, windows, weep holes, utility and plumbing penetrations, crawlspace vents, and the siding starter strip.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envuor Bifen IT (Bifenthrin) - CSI
🌪 Crawlspace and Entry Dusting

Dust crawlspace vents and entry doors without puncturing vent screens. Dust cracks and crevices behind AC units, electrical boxes, and water spigots. Dust gaps below siding and stucco.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
6
Customer Recommendations - Moisture and Harborage

Centipedes cannot persist in a dry structure with no prey. These recommendations address the actual cause and should be communicated on every centipede service.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Run a dehumidifierBasements and crawlspaces held below 50 percent relative humidity will not support a centipede population. This is the highest-impact change a customer can make.
Repair plumbing leaksLeaks under sinks, behind toilets, and at water heaters create the damp microclimate centipedes require.
Improve exterior drainageExtend downspouts away from the foundation, clear gutters, and correct grading so water moves away from the structure.
Ventilate crawlspacesAdequate ventilation or an encapsulated, conditioned crawlspace removes the humidity reservoir feeding the interior.
Remove exterior harboragePull leaf litter, thick mulch, groundcover, stone, and firewood back from the foundation. Keep mulch depth to 2 inches or less.
Reduce interior clutterCardboard boxes and stored items on basement floors hold moisture and shelter both centipedes and their prey.
Seal entry pointsCaulk gaps around plumbing penetrations, install door sweeps, and repair damaged crawlspace vent screens.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"House centipedes look alarming but they're actually predators - they're in here eating other insects. That tells me two things: you've got enough moisture to keep them alive, and there's something for them to eat. I treated the voids and harborage areas today and hit the other insects too. The biggest thing on your end is drying that basement out - a dehumidifier down there will do more than any spray I can put down."

7
Post Service

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting where activity was found, moisture conditions observed, prey insects present, and all treatment locations.

Knock on the door and report to the customer, emphasizing the moisture and prey connection. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Advise the customer they may see a temporary increase in centipede sightings immediately after treatment as insects are flushed from voids and harborage. Activity should decline noticeably over the following two weeks.

If moisture conditions are not corrected, centipedes will return. Schedule a follow-up if activity persists beyond three weeks or if the underlying moisture issue remains unresolved.
