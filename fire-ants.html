General Pest Protocol
Fire Ant Control
Protect customers and their families by eliminating fire ant colonies throughout the yard using spray and baiting methods with prevention granulation. Fire ants sting aggressively in numbers - never disturb a mound before treating it.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Fire Ant
Solenopsis invicta
Size: 1/8 to 1/4 in - workers vary in size within the same colony, which is a key identifier. Color: Reddish-brown head and thorax with a darker abdomen. Mound: Dome of loose soil with no central opening; ants exit through underground tunnels. Behavior: Swarms and stings aggressively in numbers when the mound is disturbed. Sting leaves a characteristic white pustule.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they have been seeing fire ants and how long it has been an issue. Reiterate that stinging insects are warrantied and they should not hesitate to contact us. Ask whether anyone has been stung and whether anyone in the household has a known sting allergy. Ask where children and pets play in the yard. Inquire if there is anything else they would like addressed.

If anyone reports a sting reaction, direct them to seek medical attention. Fire ants sting repeatedly and in numbers, and stings cause anaphylaxis in sensitized individuals. Do not assess a sting or offer medical guidance - refer them to a physician.
2
Identification

Confirm you are dealing with fire ants before treating. The mound structure alone is usually enough.

The Ant

1/8 to 1/4 in, reddish-brown head and thorax with a darker abdomen. Workers vary in size within the same colony - a strong identifier, since most pest ants are uniform. Two nodes and a visible stinger.

The Mound

Dome of loose, worked soil with no central opening - ants enter and exit through underground tunnels radiating outward. Mounds appear or enlarge rapidly after rain. Disturbing one triggers an immediate mass defensive response.

Colonies Relocate. Fire ant colonies move readily, especially after flooding or disturbance. A mound treated last visit may reappear a few feet away. This is normal and is why prevention granulation matters as much as mound treatment.
3
Inspection

Inspect the entire lawn for fire ant mounds. Flag each mound or make a mental note of each location.

Consider the distance from your service vehicle - are any mounds too far for your hose to reach? This determines which control method you use on each mound, so establish it before you start treating.

Pay particular attention to mounds near play areas, walkways, patios, and anywhere the family spends time. Note these for the customer conversation.

4
Control

Two methods, chosen by whether the hose reaches. Spray is preferred wherever it is possible.

💧 Spray Method (Preferred)

If your hose can reach the mound: create an escape barrier by fan spraying a circle or square, one foot away from the mound edges.

Then flood the colony by pin streaming in three points toward the cone center - think of a Mercedes emblem for your pin pattern.

This is the quickest way to eliminate the colony.

Route Mix (Pyrethroid)
🌾 Baiting Method (When the Sprayer Cannot Reach)

DO NOT DISTURB the mound by applying directly to the cone.

Distribute approximately 4 tablespoons (0.5 oz) around 3 to 4 ft around each mound.

Foragers carry the bait down to the queen and brood.

Extinguish Advion Fire Ant (Indoxacarb) - Syngenta
🛡 Prevention - Granulation

Boundary Application: Apply to soil areas, ornamental gardens, pavement crevices, fence lines, planter areas, vegetation, and mulch areas.

Lawn Application: Broadcast with suitable equipment for uniform coverage.

Perimeter Application: Apply to ground area 5 to 10 feet wide around and adjacent to the structure.

Talstar XTRA w/ Verge (Zeta-Cypermethrin, Bifenthrin) - Envu
Never disturb a mound before treating it. Kicking, probing, or spraying directly into the cone scatters the colony and can cause it to bud into several new mounds. It is also how technicians get swarmed. Treat from the outside in.
5
Communication and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting mound locations, count, which method was used on each, and products applied.

Report to the customer and reinforce the warranty. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Allow 2 weeks for complete elimination - especially for colonies that were baited, since bait must be carried to the queen before the colony collapses.

Recommend they do not disturb the colonies to avoid spreading. Mowing over, kicking, or pouring home remedies on a mound causes budding into multiple new colonies.

Follow up in 7 to 10 days to monitor results.

Advise that new mounds may appear after heavy rain as colonies relocate. This is expected and covered - stinging insects are warrantied.

Suggested Customer Talk Track

"I flagged and treated every mound I could find out here. The ones I could reach with the hose got flooded directly, and the ones further out got baited - those take a couple weeks because the workers have to carry it down to the queen. Big thing on your end: don't disturb the mounds. If you kick one or mow over it, the colony splits and you end up with three instead of one. And if you see new mounds pop up after a heavy rain, just call us - fire ants are warrantied on your service."
