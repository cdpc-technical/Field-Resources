General Pest Protocol
Hornets - European & Bald-Faced
Locate and eliminate European and bald-faced hornet colonies through direct treatment of the nest entrance, using aerosol on exposed paper nests and dust in concealed voids. Both species sting repeatedly and defend aggressively - full PPE, correct timing, and a planned retreat are mandatory.
PEAK JUL - OCT
Field Identification
Hornets
Vespa crabro / Dolichovespula maculata
European Hornet: 1 to 1.5 in, brown and dull orange-yellow with reddish-brown thorax. Nests in concealed cavities; active at night and drawn to lights. Bald-Faced Hornet: ~3/4 in, glossy black with ivory white face and abdomen tip. Builds the large exposed gray paper nest. Highly aggressive; both sting repeatedly.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where the nest is located and how high off the ground. Ask whether anyone has been stung, whether anyone in the household has a known sting allergy, and how close the nest is to doors, walkways, and play areas. Ask them to keep everyone clear of the area until you have finished.

If anyone reports a sting reaction, direct them to seek medical attention. Hornets sting repeatedly and deliver a large venom volume. Bald-faced hornets are notably aggressive in defense of the nest. Do not assess a sting or offer medical guidance - refer them to a physician.
2
Identification - European vs Bald-Faced

The two species we encounter differ substantially in nesting behavior, aggression, and how the treatment is approached. Identify which one you are dealing with before planning the treatment.

European Hornet

Large, roughly 1 to 1.5 inches. Brown and dull orange-yellow with a reddish-brown thorax. The largest wasp encountered in most of our markets.

Nests in concealed cavities - hollow trees, wall voids, attics, soffits, barns, and outbuildings. Nest is rarely fully visible.

Active at night and attracted to lights, which is unusual among wasps and a frequent customer complaint. Will strike lit windows after dark.

Bald-Faced Hornet

Roughly 3/4 inch, glossy black with distinctive ivory white markings on the face and the end of the abdomen. Technically a large yellow jacket rather than a true hornet.

Builds the large exposed gray paper nest - the classic football or teardrop shape hanging in a tree, shrub, or under an eave. Nest may reach basketball size by late season.

Highly aggressive near the nest and will pursue an intruder a considerable distance. The most defensive stinging pest we treat.

Shared Biology

Colonies are annual. A single overwintered queen founds the nest in spring and the colony builds through the season, peaking in late summer and early fall.

They do not lose their stinger and can sting repeatedly. Both species deliver a substantially larger venom dose than a paper wasp.

Nests are abandoned in winter and are not reused the following year, though the same favorable locations attract new queens.

European Hornets and Tree Damage: European hornets strip bark from shrubs and thin-barked trees such as lilac, birch, and ash to gather nest material and feed on sap. Customers frequently report this damage without connecting it to the hornets.
3
Inspection and Nest Location

Locate the nest and assess whether it can be treated safely before committing to the job.

Bald-Faced Hornet - Exposed Nest

The nest is visible. Look in trees and large shrubs, under eaves and overhangs, on soffits and gable ends, under decks, and on outbuildings and fence structures.

Locate the single entrance hole near the bottom of the paper envelope. This is where treatment is delivered.

Assess height and access. Note whether a ladder is required and whether there is a clear retreat path.

European Hornet - Concealed Nest

The nest is hidden, so watch flight lines to a single entry point. Check hollow trees and stumps, wall voids, attic and soffit voids, barns, sheds, and under porch decking.

Look for steady directional traffic at a fixed opening. Check around exterior lighting after dark, since this species is drawn to lights.

Where the nest is in a wall or attic void, treat it as a void nest and follow the void treatment guidance below.

Do not disturb, probe, or strike the nest during inspection. Bald-faced hornets will launch a mass defensive response and pursue. Observe from a distance and plan your approach and exit before moving in.
Know when to decline. If the nest is too high to reach safely, requires working from a ladder in a position you cannot retreat from, or is in a confined attic space with a single exit, stop and escalate to your Service Manager. No nest is worth a fall or a mass sting incident.
4
Treatment

Treat the nest directly at its entrance. Both species require full protective equipment and correct timing.

🕑 Timing

Treat at dusk, after dark, or in early morning. The full colony is present and inactive and the defensive response is far weaker.

Midday treatment leaves foragers in the field and provokes maximum aggression. Avoid it wherever possible.

European hornets are active at night, so early morning is generally the better window for that species. Use a red-filtered light rather than white light where working after dark, as white light draws them.

💧 Bald-Faced Hornet - Exposed Nest

Approach from below and to the side, never directly beneath the entrance. Apply a contact aerosol with a jet stream directly into the entrance hole at the bottom of the nest, delivering a sustained application.

Step back immediately and observe from a safe distance. Do not remove the nest at time of treatment.

Return the following day, confirm no activity, then remove and dispose of the nest. Removing a live nest is how technicians get stung.

Contact Wasp & Hornet Aerosol (Jet Stream)
🌪 Void and Concealed Nest

Apply D-Fense Dust into the void through the entry point using a bulb duster with an extension tip. Workers track dust deep into the nest structure, which is why dust outperforms liquid in a void.

Do not seal the entry point at time of treatment. Sealing traps live hornets that will chew through into interior living space. Advise the customer to wait until activity has fully ceased before sealing.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
💧 Perimeter and Follow-Up Residual

Following nest treatment, apply a liquid residual around the nest area, along eaves, soffits, fascia, and around doors, windows, and exterior light fixtures to discourage re-nesting and reduce foraging.

For European hornet accounts, treat around exterior lighting specifically, since that is where activity concentrates.

Temprid FX (Beta-Cyfluthrin, Imidacloprid) - Envuor Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
Full PPE is mandatory for any hornet nest treatment. Bee suit, veil, and gloves. Plan and clear your retreat path before you approach. Never work directly beneath an exposed nest, and never treat from a ladder position you cannot exit quickly.
5
Customer Recommendations

Prevention focuses on early season nest detection and reducing the conditions that attract foraging.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Check for new nests in late springAn early season nest is golf ball sized with few workers and is far safer and easier to treat than a mature late season nest.
Seal structural gaps before springCaulk siding seams and trim gaps, screen attic and gable vents, and seal soffit openings before queens begin nesting.
Reduce or shield exterior lightingEuropean hornets are drawn to lights at night. Yellow bulbs, motion fixtures, or relocating lights away from doors reduces activity.
Keep trash sealed and fruit picked upLate season foragers scavenge protein and sugar. Tight lids and clearing fallen fruit removes the attraction.
Remove old nests in winterNests are not reused, but removing them in winter once the colony is dead prevents confusion and reassures the customer.
Never attempt DIY removalHomeowners knocking down or spraying a mature nest is the leading cause of multiple sting incidents. Emphasize this clearly.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"That's a bald-faced hornet nest, and they're about the most defensive thing we deal with - they'll come after you a good distance if you bump that nest. I'm going to treat it right at dusk when they're all home and settled. I'm leaving the nest hanging tonight; I'll take it down once I've confirmed there's no activity, because pulling a live nest is exactly how people get stung. Whatever you do, don't let anybody try to knock it down or spray it themselves."

6
Post Service

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting the species, nest location and height, treatment time of day, product applied, and whether the nest was removed or left for follow-up.

Report to the customer, emphasizing that the area should be avoided until activity ceases, that the nest will be removed on a return visit if applicable, and that void entry points must not be sealed yet. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations and Follow-Up

Advise the customer that activity typically ceases within 24 to 72 hours. Returning foragers will continue arriving for a day or two after treatment.

Schedule a return visit to confirm the colony is dead and remove an exposed nest. Never remove a nest on the same visit as treatment.

For void nests, advise sealing only after activity has fully stopped, and note the location so the cavity can be addressed.

If activity continues beyond a week, a second treatment may be required on a large late season colony.
