General Pest Protocol
Common House Spiders
Reduce house spider populations and webbing through thorough interior and exterior de-webbing and egg sac removal, targeted treatment of eaves, soffits and light fixture zones, and reduction of the exterior lighting and insect prey that draw them in. Screen for black widow and brown recluse during inspection.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Common House Spiders
Parasteatoda, Pholcus & related

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing webs and spiders, inside and out, and how quickly webs return after being knocked down. Ask whether outdoor lights are left on overnight. Ask whether anyone is fearful of spiders or has been bitten, and whether they have seen any spiders they are concerned about specifically.

If the customer reports a suspected bite, direct them to seek medical attention. Do not assess the bite or identify a spider from a wound. If black widow or brown recluse is suspected during inspection, refer to those protocols - they are handled differently.
2
Identification

General house spider service covers the common web-building species. Your first job is confirming you are not dealing with a medically significant species.

Common House Spider

~1/4 in, tan to brown with a rounded, mottled abdomen and faint chevron markings. Builds a tangled cobweb in upper corners, around windows and behind furniture. Papery tan egg sacs suspended in the web.

Cellar Spider

Small body with extremely long, thin legs - the "daddy long-legs" of basements and garages. Loose, irregular, messy web. Vibrates rapidly in the web when disturbed. Completely harmless.

Both Are Web-Sitting and Non-Aggressive. Neither is medically significant. They rarely leave the web and will not pursue. Most of what customers report as a "spider problem" is really a webbing problem.
Screen for the Two That Matter. While inspecting, watch for a red hourglass on a glossy black abdomen (black widow) or a dark violin marking on a uniform tan spider (brown recluse). If either is found, switch to that protocol - both require harborage-focused void dusting and different customer guidance.
3
Inspection

Inspect the full exterior and the affected interior areas with a flashlight, working top to bottom. Web location tells you where to focus treatment.

Exterior

Around exterior lighting - porch lights, garage coach lights, floodlights and landscape lighting. This is where webbing will be heaviest.

Eaves, soffits, fascia, gable ends and the roofline junction. Corners of door and window frames, shutters, and porch ceilings and columns.

Deck railings and undersides, pergolas, playsets, patio furniture, mailboxes, downspouts, and the spans between shrubs and the structure.

Note prey accumulation in and around light fixtures - insects are why the spiders are there.

Interior

Upper wall and ceiling corners, around window frames, behind and under furniture, and around ceiling light fixtures.

Basements, garages, crawlspaces and storage areas - especially cellar spiders in cool damp corners.

Check for egg sacs suspended in webbing. Remove them along with the web.

Entry Points

Door sweeps and thresholds, garage door seals, window screens, weep holes, utility penetrations and crawlspace vents.

4
Control - De-Webbing

Mechanical removal is the primary control and it is what the customer judges the service on. Removing the web removes the spider's home, its egg sacs and the visible complaint all at once.

🧹 Exterior De-Webbing

Dust the de-web head with D-Fense Dust prior to de-webbing (do this in a grassy area and be careful of drift), then de-web the full structure using the Cotton Candy Method.

De-web all corners, doors, windows, soffits, below siding, downspouts, above and below decks, around lights and around the mailbox.

Use an extension pole to reach upper soffits, gable ends and second-story eaves. Spiders build high, and a ground-level-only pass leaves the worst webbing in place.

Remove and dispose of egg sacs rather than knocking them to the ground.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
🧹 Interior De-Webbing and Vacuuming

De-web interior corners, ceilings, window frames and behind furniture in affected rooms.

Where a customer prefers, recommend vacuuming webs, spiders and egg sacs - it removes everything immediately and cleanly. Advise disposing of the vacuum contents promptly.

Be Thorough Here: The customer judges this service almost entirely on whether the webs are gone when they walk outside. Time spent de-webbing produces more perceived value than any other part of the visit.
5
Control - Chemical Treatment

Residual treatment discourages rebuilding on cleared surfaces and reduces the flying insect prey that draws spiders to the structure.

💧 Exterior Power-Spray

Treat around and behind all exterior light fixtures, eaves, soffits, fascia, gable ends and rooflines - the zones where webbing concentrates.

Treat around all door and window frames, porch ceilings and corners, columns, deck railings and underside, and around the mailbox and downspouts.

Treat the foundation 3 ft up and 4 ft out, plus landscape beds and shrubs adjacent to the structure where webs span from vegetation to wall.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envuor Bifen IT (Bifenthrin) - CSI
💧 Interior Application

With the B&G on a fine fan spray, treat lightly along baseboards, in floor-level and upper corners of affected rooms, around window and door frames, and along garage perimeters.

Do not broadcast the interior. Spiders are not feeding on treated surfaces the way a crawling insect does, so placement matters more than volume.

Onslaught FastCap (Multi Active) - MGK
🌪 Crack and Crevice Dusting

Dust soffit and fascia gaps, behind shutters, porch ceiling corners and column voids, around light fixture housings, garage floor-level corners, and voids below siding where spiders retreat and place egg sacs.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Set Realistic Chemical Expectations: A web-sitting spider rests on its own silk rather than the treated surface, so residual contact is limited compared to a ground-hunting spider. De-webbing plus reducing insects and lighting is what actually holds - the spray supports it rather than carrying it alone.
6
Customer Recommendations

House spider pressure is driven almost entirely by the insects drawn to the home at night. These changes address the cause.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Switch to yellow or sodium bulbsStandard white bulbs attract far more flying insects. Cutting the prey supply is the single most effective change available.
Turn off unnecessary exterior lightsLights left on overnight are the primary driver of webbing around entryways.
Close blinds and curtains at nightInterior light spilling through windows draws insects to the glass and produces webbing in window corners.
Knock down new webs promptlyA broom or hose on fresh webbing between services discourages rebuilding in high-traffic spots.
Vacuum interior webs and egg sacsRemoves every life stage at once. Dispose of the vacuum contents promptly.
Reduce clutter in storage areasBoxes and stored items in basements and garages provide undisturbed web sites.
Trim vegetation off the structureRemoves the anchor points spiders use to span webs to the wall.
Install door sweeps and repair screensReduces the wandering spiders that get inside at ground level.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are common house spiders and cellar spiders - completely harmless, and honestly they're eating other bugs for you. The webs are the real complaint, so I've de-webbed the whole house and treated up around the soffits and light fixtures. Here's the thing though: they build where the bugs are, and the bugs are there because of the lights. Swapping those porch bulbs to yellow ones will cut this down more than anything else. Some webbing will come back - they rebuild constantly - so knocking new ones down with a broom between visits keeps it in check."

7
Post Service and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting where webbing was heaviest, egg sacs removed, species observed, lighting conditions, and all areas de-webbed and treated.

Report to the customer, walking them around to show the de-webbing results. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Advise the customer that some webbing will return, particularly around lights left on overnight. This is normal and does not indicate a treatment failure - spiders rebuild constantly and new ones move in from surrounding vegetation.

Populations peak in late summer and early fall when spiders reach full size and webs become most noticeable.

Regular de-webbing on the quarterly route is what keeps these accounts satisfied. Where pressure is heavy, discuss more frequent service during peak season.

If a black widow or brown recluse is found at any point, contact us - those are treated differently and require a targeted harborage approach.
