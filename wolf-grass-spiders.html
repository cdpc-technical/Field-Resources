General Pest Protocol
Wolf & Grass Spiders
Reduce wolf and grass spider populations through de-webbing and funnel web removal, a thorough ground-level exterior barrier and granular application, and reduction of the harborage and exterior lighting that sustain them. Both are beneficial, non-medically-significant hunters that are routinely misreported as brown recluse.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Wolf & Grass Spiders
Lycosidae & Agelenopsis spp.
Wolf Spider: 1/2 to 1.5 in, stout, hairy, brown to gray with darker stripes. Does not build a capture web - hunts on the ground. Eyes in three rows and highly reflective under a flashlight. Female carries a round egg sac on her spinnerets. Grass Spider: ~1/2 to 3/4 in, slimmer, with two dark stripes on the cephalothorax and long, prominent spinnerets. Builds a flat sheet web with a funnel retreat in turf, shrubs and ground cover.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing the spiders and whether it is mostly indoors or outdoors. Ask whether anyone has been bitten and whether anyone is fearful of spiders, since that shapes how you set expectations. Ask about landscaping right against the foundation, exterior lighting left on overnight, and any funnel-shaped webs in the turf or shrubs.

2
Identification

Both are large, fast, ground-level hunters that alarm customers. Correct identification matters because both are routinely misreported as brown recluse, and the treatment approach differs between the two.

Wolf Spider

1/2 to 1.5 in, stout and hairy, brown to gray with darker stripes. Does not build a capture web - actively hunts on the ground.

Eyes in three rows with two large forward-facing eyes. Highly reflective under a flashlight - eyeshine at night is the fastest way to locate them. Female carries a round egg sac attached to her spinnerets, then carries spiderlings on her back.

Grass Spider

1/2 to 3/4 in, slimmer and faster, brown with two dark stripes running down the cephalothorax.

Long, prominent spinnerets extending off the rear are the giveaway. Builds a flat sheet web with a funnel retreat at one edge, in turf, groundcover, shrubs and along foundations. Sits in the funnel and darts out onto the sheet.

Not Brown Recluse. Wolf and grass spiders are far more commonly reported as recluse than actual recluse are. A recluse is uniform tan with no leg banding or body striping, has a dark violin marking on the cephalothorax, six eyes in three pairs, and is roughly 1/4 to 1/2 in. If it is large, hairy, striped and fast, it is not a recluse.
Both Are Beneficial and Non-Aggressive. Neither is medically significant. They feed on other insects and will retreat rather than confront. Reassuring a fearful customer is often the highest-value part of this visit.
3
Inspection

These are exterior, ground-level spiders. Interior sightings are almost always wanderers that came in at ground level, not an established indoor population.

Exterior - Grass Spider Webs

Look for flat sheet webs with a funnel at one corner in turf, groundcover, low shrubs, along foundation lines, in window wells, and on retaining walls and steps.

Webs are most visible in early morning with dew on them. Note how many and where they cluster.

Exterior - Wolf Spider Harborage

Ground-level cover: mulch beds, leaf litter, stacked stone, landscape timbers, firewood, debris piles, under decks and steps, and along foundation edges.

Use a flashlight at dusk or after dark. Wolf spider eyeshine reflects brightly and is by far the fastest way to gauge the population and locate harborage.

Entry Points and Interior

Door thresholds and sweeps, garage door seals, window wells, basement windows, crawlspace vents and access doors, and utility penetrations at ground level.

Interior activity concentrates in garages, basements, crawlspaces and ground-floor rooms - along baseboards, in floor-level corners and behind stored items.

Assess Prey and Lighting

Note exterior lighting left on overnight and any insect accumulation. Both spiders follow their prey, so heavy insect pressure at the structure sustains them.

4
Control - Exterior

The exterior is where this service is won. Treat the ground-level harborage band, not just the wall.

🧹 De-Webbing

Dust the de-web head with D-Fense Dust prior to de-webbing (do this in a grassy area and be careful of drift), then de-web the full structure using the Cotton Candy Method.

Physically remove grass spider funnel webs from shrubs, foundation lines, window wells, steps and retaining walls. Removing the web removes the spider's ambush position and shows the customer immediate results.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
💧 Power-Spray Barrier

Treat the foundation 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground. Fan spray mulch beds, groundcover and low shrubs heavily so product reaches below the surface where wolf spiders harbor and grass spiders build.

Treat around all doors, thresholds, garage door seals, windows, window wells, weep holes, crawlspace vents and utility penetrations.

Treat under decks and steps, around porch skirting, and into stone and timber harborage.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envuor Bifen IT (Bifenthrin) - CSI
🌾 Granular Application

Apply granule around the structure, walking 7 to 9 ft from the foundation. Focus on mulch beds, groundcover and turf edges. Granule carries into the thatch and litter layer where both species live and hunt.

Wisdom EZ or Wisdom G L/N (Bifenthrin) - per seasonal rotation
🌪 Harborage Dusting

Dust voids in stacked stone and retaining walls, foundation cracks, weep holes, window wells and behind the siding starter strip.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
5
Control - Interior

Interior work targets wanderers at ground level. Neither species establishes an indoor population, so this is targeted rather than broadcast.

💧 Targeted Application

With the B&G on a fine fan spray, treat along baseboards in affected rooms, floor-level corners, behind and under stored items, around plumbing penetrations, and along garage perimeters and door seals.

Concentrate on garages, basements, crawlspaces and ground-floor rooms where activity was reported.

Onslaught FastCap (Multi Active) - MGK
📌 Glue Board Monitoring

Place glue boards at floor level in corners and along walls in garages, basements and crawlspaces. These capture wandering spiders and give a clear read on how many are getting in and where.

Place out of reach of pets and children.

Trapper Max Glue Boards - Bell Labs
6
Customer Recommendations

Harborage reduction and lighting changes are what keep these populations down long term.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Switch to yellow exterior bulbsLights draw the insects both spiders hunt. Cutting the prey supply is the single most effective long-term change.
Thin mulch and clear leaf litterRemoves the ground-level harborage wolf spiders shelter in and grass spiders anchor webs to.
Pull groundcover off the foundationDense groundcover and low shrubs against the wall are prime funnel web sites and a direct bridge to the structure.
Remove stone, timber and debris pilesStacked material against the foundation is ideal wolf spider harborage.
Move firewood away and elevate itFirewood in soil contact near the home shelters both spiders and their prey.
Install door sweeps and seal gapsThese are ground-level spiders. Door sweeps, garage seals and sealed foundation cracks stop most interior wandering.
Knock down funnel webs between visitsRemoving webs promptly discourages grass spiders from re-establishing in high-traffic areas.
Keep grass cut and edges trimmedTall turf and unmaintained edges are where grass spiders build. Regular mowing reduces available sites.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"Good news first - these aren't brown recluse. That's the number one thing people call these in as. A recluse is small, plain tan, no stripes on the legs. What you've got are wolf spiders and grass spiders, and they're both harmless hunters that eat other bugs. They live at ground level, so the ones getting inside are just wandering under the door. I treated the mulch beds and the foundation band and pulled the funnel webs down. If you can swap those porch bulbs to yellow, that cuts the insects they're hunting and you'll see a real difference."

7
Post Service and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting which species were found, funnel web locations and count, harborage conditions observed, glue board placements and all areas treated.

Report to the customer, leading with the fact that these are not brown recluse and are not medically significant. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Advise the customer they may see spiders for a period after treatment as they contact the residual and are flushed from harborage.

Wolf spiders will continue wandering in from surrounding property, particularly in fall as they seek shelter. Treatment reduces pressure but cannot prevent all movement from off site.

Grass spider webs may reappear. Regular de-webbing on the quarterly route is what keeps these accounts clear.

Check glue boards on the follow-up visit - counts are the clearest measure of whether interior entry is declining.
