General Pest Protocol
Cicada Killer Wasp
Eliminate cicada killer burrows through direct dust treatment of each nest entrance, supported by surface and granular application, and correct the bare turf conditions that attract them. These are solitary, non-aggressive wasps - the males customers fear cannot sting at all.
PEAK JUL - AUG
Field Identification
Cicada Killer Wasp
Sphecius speciosus
Size: 1.5 to 2 in - one of the largest wasps in North America. Color: Rusty red head and thorax, black abdomen with broken yellow bands, amber wings. Nest: Solitary burrow in bare, well-drained soil with a U-shaped mound of excavated dirt at the opening. Behavior: Males hover and dive-bomb aggressively but have no stinger. Females sting only if handled or trapped.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing the wasps and whether they have noticed holes in the ground with small mounds of loose dirt. Ask how long it has been occurring and whether anyone has been stung. Ask where children and pets play in the yard, and whether the area has thin turf, bare patches, or sandy soil.

Reiterate that stinging insects are warrantied and they should not hesitate to contact us.

2
Identification and Biology

Cicada killers alarm customers more than almost any stinging insect we handle, purely because of their size. Being able to explain the biology confidently is the most valuable thing you do on this call.

Male - The One Customers See

Patrols the nesting area, hovers, and dive-bombs anything that moves including people and lawn mowers. This is pure territorial bluffing. The male has no stinger and physically cannot sting.

Female - The One That Digs

Larger, does all the burrowing and hunting. Focused entirely on excavating and provisioning. Extremely reluctant to sting and will generally only do so if grabbed or trapped against skin.

Solitary, Not Social: Cicada killers do not form colonies and do not defend a nest collectively the way yellow jackets or hornets do. Many burrows in one area is many individual females sharing good soil, not one large colony. There is no nest to eliminate.
Life Cycle and Season

Adults emerge in July and August, timed to the cicada emergence, and are active for roughly 6 to 10 weeks.

The female stings and paralyzes a cicada, carries it into her burrow, and lays an egg on it. The larva feeds through the fall and overwinters underground, emerging as an adult the following summer.

One generation per year. Activity ends on its own by early fall regardless of treatment.

Distinguish From European Hornet: European hornets are similar in size but have a reddish-brown thorax with a more uniformly banded yellow abdomen, nest in cavities rather than the ground, and are genuinely aggressive. Cicada killers nest in bare soil with a mound at the entrance and are not.
3
Inspection

Locate and count the burrows. Each burrow is one female, so the burrow count tells you the actual scale of the job.

Where to Look

Bare or thin turf in full sun with well-drained or sandy soil. This is the defining condition - they will not dig in dense healthy turf.

Slopes and embankments, edges of sidewalks, driveways, patios, and along retaining walls.

Sand traps and bare infield areas on athletic fields and golf courses.

Under decks, along foundations with exposed soil, in flower beds with bare ground, and areas where turf has thinned from drought or shade.

What a Burrow Looks Like

A hole roughly the diameter of a quarter with a U-shaped or horseshoe mound of excavated soil fanned out at the entrance.

You may see a female dragging a paralyzed cicada toward the opening, or males patrolling low over the area.

Flag or note each burrow location before treating so none are missed.

4
Treatment

Treat each burrow directly. Dust is the preferred material because the female carries it deep into the tunnel on her body as she enters and exits.

🕑 Timing

Treat at dusk, after dark, or in early morning when females are inside the burrow and activity is lowest.

Midday treatment leaves females out foraging who will return to a treated entrance - still effective, but slower and with more patrolling males around you.

🌪 Burrow Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust directly into each burrow entrance using a bulb duster with an extension tip. Deliver a generous application into the opening.

Do not plug or cover the hole. The female must move through the treated entrance to carry dust down to the brood chamber.

Treat every burrow found. A missed burrow is a female that completes her cycle and seeds next summer's population in the same spot.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
💧 Surface and Perimeter Treatment

Following burrow treatment, apply a liquid residual across the nesting area and immediately surrounding turf and soil to treat females landing and walking on the surface.

Also treat along adjacent hardscape edges, foundation lines, and retaining walls where burrows cluster.

Temprid FX (Beta-Cyfluthrin, Imidacloprid) - Envuor Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
🌾 Granular Application

Apply granule across the affected turf and soil areas to provide extended residual in the nesting zone, particularly where burrows are numerous or spread across a wide area.

Wisdom EZ or Wisdom G L/N (Bifenthrin) - per seasonal rotation
PPE and positioning: Wear full PPE and be aware that males will hover and dive at you while you work. They cannot sting, but the behavior is startling on a ladder or slope. Keep your footing and do not react abruptly on uneven ground.
5
Customer Recommendations - Turf and Soil

Cicada killers are a turf condition problem. They require bare, well-drained soil to dig. Correcting the lawn is what prevents them returning next July, and chemical treatment alone will not.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Thicken thin and bare turfThe single most effective long-term fix. Females will not dig through dense healthy turf. Overseed and fertilize bare patches.
Water regularly during the seasonThey strongly prefer dry, well-drained soil. Consistent irrigation through July and August makes the site far less attractive.
Mulch or plant bare soil areasCovering exposed soil in beds and along hardscape removes the digging surface entirely.
Address drainage and slope erosionEroded slopes and washed-out edges create the bare, well-drained conditions they seek.
Correct shade-thinned turfThinning turf under trees creates ideal patches. Prune to increase light or convert to bed and mulch.
Do not swat at the malesThey cannot sting. Reassure the customer so they are not panicking every time they step outside.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"I know these things look terrifying - they're one of the biggest wasps in the country. But here's the thing: the ones buzzing at you when you walk out are the males, and they physically don't have a stinger. They're all bluff. The females are the ones digging those holes, and they're only interested in cicadas. They're not a colony either - every hole is one individual female. I treated all the burrows I found. The real fix is that thin patch of grass - they can only dig where the soil's bare and dry, so thickening that turf up is what keeps them from coming back next July."

6
Communication and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting burrow count and locations, turf and soil conditions observed, products applied, and all recommendations made.

Report to the customer, leading with the fact that males cannot sting. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Activity typically declines within several days as females contact the treated burrow entrances.

Some patrolling males will remain visible for a period after treatment. They are harmless and will die off naturally as the season ends.

Follow up in 7 to 10 days to check for new burrows and retreat as needed. Females will re-dig if suitable bare soil remains.

Activity ends naturally by early fall. Where turf conditions are not corrected, expect them to return to the same area the following July - schedule proactively.
