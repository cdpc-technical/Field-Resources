1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing the moths and whether they have found webbing or larvae in any food product. Ask how long it has been occurring. Ask specifically about the pantry, cabinets, dry pet food, bird seed, and any bulk dry goods. Ask whether they have recently purchased bulk grain, flour, rice, nuts, or pet food.

Explain up front that this service depends on locating and discarding the infested product, and that you will need access to the pantry and food storage areas.

2
Identification and Biology

Indian meal moth is the most common stored product pest in residential accounts. Understanding the life cycle explains why customers keep seeing moths after they think they have solved it.

Adult Moth

About 1/2 inch wingspan. The forewing is two-toned - pale gray or cream on the inner half and coppery reddish-brown on the outer half. This banded appearance is definitive. Flies in an erratic, fluttering pattern, typically at dusk.

Larva - The Damaging Stage

Off-white to cream caterpillar up to 1/2 inch with a brown head capsule. Produces silken webbing through infested product - clumped, matted food with fine silk is the definitive sign. Larvae wander well away from the food source to pupate.

Why Customers Keep Seeing Moths

The larva crawls away from the food source to pupate, often traveling to ceiling corners, wall and ceiling junctions, the tops of door frames, and inside cabinet hinges - frequently in a different room entirely.

This is why moths continue appearing after the customer discards the obvious infested package. Pupae scattered around the structure keep emerging for weeks.

The complete life cycle runs roughly 6 to 8 weeks under normal household conditions, and faster in warm conditions.

Larvae chew through plastic bags, thin cardboard, and paper packaging. Sealed original packaging is not protection.

They Almost Always Come In With Groceries. Indian meal moth infestations typically originate from product already infested at purchase or from a bulk item stored too long. Explaining this to the customer removes the stigma and shifts the conversation to finding the source.
3
Inspection and Source Identification

Finding and removing the infested product is the service. Chemical work supports it but cannot substitute for it. Be thorough - the source is frequently an item the customer forgot they had.

Inspect Every Dry Product

Check all dry goods, not just the obvious ones: flour, cornmeal, rice, pasta, cereal, oats, crackers, dried fruit, nuts, seeds, spices, chocolate, candy, powdered drink mix, and baking supplies.

Commonly missed sources: dry pet food, bird seed, grass seed, decorative dried flowers and Indian corn, potpourri, rodent bait, and forgotten bulk items at the back of a pantry.

Look for webbing, clumped or matted product, larvae, shed skins, and frass. Pour product into a light-colored bowl where uncertain.

Check inside, under, and behind all containers. Inspect the seams and lids of storage bins.

Inspect the Structure, Not Just the Food

Check pantry and cabinet corners, shelf edges, and the underside of shelving. Inspect cabinet hinges and the void behind cabinet frames.

Check wall and ceiling junctions, ceiling corners, the tops of door and window frames, and behind hanging pictures - these are where wandering larvae pupate.

Check adjacent rooms. Pupae are frequently found well away from the food source.

Every infested product must be discarded. Do not attempt to salvage. Bag infested items, seal the bag, and have the customer remove it to the outdoor bin immediately - not to an indoor trash can where adults can emerge and reinfest.
4
Source Removal and Sanitation

Physical removal and cleaning eliminate the population. This step does more than any product applied.

Removal and Cleaning Sequence

Have the customer discard all infested product into sealed bags removed to the exterior bin.

Completely empty the affected pantry and cabinets. Partial removal leaves eggs and pupae behind.

Vacuum thoroughly - shelves, shelf edges, corners, cracks, hinges, and the floor beneath. Vacuuming physically removes eggs, larvae, and pupae and is the single most effective sanitation step. Dispose of the vacuum bag or empty the canister outside immediately.

Wipe shelving with soap and water. Allow surfaces to dry fully before restocking.

Advise transferring all retained dry goods into hard-sided airtight containers - glass, metal, or heavy plastic. Larvae chew through bags and thin cardboard.

Freezing Salvageable Product

Where the customer wishes to keep an unopened item that may have been exposed, advise freezing it at 0 degrees F for a minimum of 4 to 7 days to kill all life stages, then storing it in an airtight container.

5
Xlure RTU Pheromone Trap Placement

ClearDefense uses Xlure RTU pheromone traps for Indian meal moth. The trap uses a species-specific pheromone to attract and capture adult male moths, which both reduces mating and provides the monitoring data that tells you whether the source was actually eliminated.

📌 Placement

Place traps in and near the affected pantry, cabinets, and food storage areas. Position at or slightly above the level of the stored product where practical.

Place traps away from strong air currents, HVAC vents, fans, and direct sunlight, all of which disrupt the pheromone plume.

Use multiple traps where the infestation spans more than one room or storage area. Also place traps in adjacent rooms where moths have been sighted, since pupae scatter widely.

Do not place traps directly against open food. Keep them out of reach of children and pets.

Xlure RTU Pheromone Trap
📈 Monitoring Value

Trap counts are your diagnostic tool. Record the count at every visit.

Declining counts over successive visits confirm the source was eliminated and the population is dying out.

Counts that stay level or rise mean an infested product was missed. Re-inspect thoroughly rather than simply replacing the trap and retreating.

Replace traps per label interval, as the pheromone lure depletes over time. Note replacement dates in FieldRoutes.

Explain the Trap Honestly: Xlure RTU captures male moths. It reduces mating and tells us whether the problem is resolving, but it will not by itself eliminate an infestation while an infested product remains in the home. Set that expectation so the customer does not treat the trap as the whole solution.
6
Chemical Treatment

Chemical application is limited, targeted, and applied only to emptied and cleaned surfaces. It handles larvae and pupae hiding in cracks that vacuuming missed.

💧 Crack and Crevice Application

Apply only after the pantry and cabinets have been emptied, vacuumed, and cleaned. Never treat with food present.

Treat cracks and crevices in shelving, shelf edges, cabinet corners and seams, hinge voids, and the junction where shelving meets the wall.

Allow surfaces to dry completely before the customer restocks. Advise lining shelves with fresh paper where preferred.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envu
🌪 Void Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust into inaccessible voids behind cabinet frames, in hinge voids, and along wall and shelf junctions where larvae pupate and liquid cannot reach.

Apply a light, even coating. Do not apply where dust could contact food or food-contact surfaces.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Never apply product to food, food-contact surfaces, or areas where food is stored unprotected. All product must be applied to emptied, cleaned surfaces and be fully dry before restocking. Confirm the site is on the product label.

Customer Recommendations

Prevention is straightforward and entirely within the customer's control. Cover each point before leaving.

Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are Indian meal moths and they came in with something you bought - that's almost always how it happens, so don't feel bad about it. The key thing is that the larvae crawl away from the food to spin their cocoon, up in ceiling corners and door frames, sometimes in a whole different room. That's why you'll keep seeing moths for a few weeks even after we pull the bad product. I've set pheromone traps - those catch the males and, more importantly, they tell me whether we actually got the source. If those counts don't drop by my next visit, something's still in here and we'll go looking again."

8
Post Service and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting the infested product identified and discarded, all areas inspected and treated, trap locations and initial counts, and all recommendations made.

Report to the customer, emphasizing that moths will continue emerging for several weeks from pupae already in place, and that trap counts are how we confirm success. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations and Follow-Up

Advise the customer that moths will continue to appear for several weeks after the source is removed, as pupae already scattered through the structure complete development. This is expected and is not a treatment failure.

Because the life cycle runs 6 to 8 weeks, schedule follow-up at 2 to 4 week intervals to check trap counts, replace traps per label, and reinspect.

If trap counts are not declining by the second visit, an infested product was missed. Conduct a full re-inspection including pet food, bird seed, and decorative items rather than simply retreating.

Do not declare control until trap counts have dropped to zero and remained there across consecutive visits.
