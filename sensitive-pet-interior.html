ClearDefense Pest Control  /  Service Protocol

Sensitive Pet Households

Birds  •  Reptiles  •  Amphibians  •  Aquariums and Aquatic Invertebrates

Applies to: All Residential and Commercial Interior Service Audience: Technicians, Chiefs, Service Managers, GMs Rev. 2026

Standard interior service assumes a mammal household. It is not safe for birds, herps, or aquatic systems. The residue is rarely what harms these animals. The application event is: airborne droplets, drifting dust, propellant vapor, and wet surfaces the animal contacts before cure. This protocol governs how we adjust baseboard applications, interior dusting, and aerosol use when any of these animals are in the structure.

Compliance basis
Nearly every pyrethroid and pyrethrin label we carry contains a precautionary statement directing the applicator to remove pets and birds and to cover fish aquariums before application. Under FIFRA the label is law. Skipping these steps is a misuse violation regardless of whether the animal is harmed.

01Universal Prohibitions

Never, in any structure covered by this protocol
  1. No total release foggers (bug bombs). No exceptions, no customer waiver.
  2. No space treatment, ULV, or fogging of any kind, interior or attached garage.
  3. No aerosol released into open air. Crack and crevice straw only, and only under Section 06.
  4. No baseboard application while the animal is in the room. Pets out of the room, product applied, full dry time before they return. See Section 04 and Section 07.
  5. No surface dusting. Sealed void injection only.
  6. No liquid, dust, or aerosol in the room where the animal or tank is located. Treat that room with bait or mechanical measures only.
  7. No application while an aquarium or screen-top enclosure is uncovered.

Default strategy in these homes is exterior-first. Perimeter, entry points, harborage, and exclusion carry the account. Interior work drops to baits, IGR point sources, monitors, and targeted crack and crevice. If you find yourself needing broad interior coverage, that is an inspection and exclusion failure, not a product problem.

02Risk by Animal Class

BirdsTier 1 - Highest

Primary route: Inhalation

Birds use a unidirectional air sac system with cross-current gas exchange. It is dramatically more efficient than a mammal lung, which means a bird extracts far more of any airborne contaminant per breath than a person or a dog in the same room. This is why canaries went into mines. Airborne particulate, solvent vapor, and propellant are all in play. Small species and any bird in a cage near the floor are at the highest exposure.

Secondary route is contact and ingestion. Free-flighted birds land on baseboards, sills, and door casings, then preen. Toucans, parrots, and other beak-active species chew what they land on.

Handling: bird leaves the structure entirely before treating. Another room is not sufficient. Coordinate at scheduling so the customer is not scrambling on arrival. Tropical species are thermally fragile, so a hot garage or a cold vehicle is not an acceptable holding spot.

AmphibiansTier 1 - Highest

Primary route: Dermal absorption and cutaneous respiration

Frogs, toads, newts, salamanders, and axolotls breathe and drink through permeable skin. They absorb dissolved and deposited contaminants directly with no barrier and limited ability to metabolize them. Most setups also include standing water, so they carry the aquarium risk at the same time. High-humidity enclosures hold deposited mist rather than letting it dry off.

Handling: treat as a bird and a fish tank combined. Enclosure leaves the structure if portable. If it is not portable, the room is a no-treat zone with the door closed and towel-sealed at the threshold for the duration of service.

Aquariums, Ponds, and Aquatic InvertebratesTier 1 - Highest

Primary route: Airborne deposition, plus mechanical injection via air pump

Pyrethroids are acutely toxic to fish and aquatic invertebrates in the parts-per-billion range. Shrimp, crayfish, snails, and reef invertebrates are more sensitive than the fish. A drift level that is meaningless to a person is lethal to the tank.

The vector techs miss is the air pump. An aquarium air pump pulls room air and bubbles it straight through the water column. Protein skimmers and hang-on-back filters do the same thing at the surface. Leaving them running during an application is a direct injection line.

Handling: cover the tank with plastic sheeting, shut off the air pump and any skimmer or open-top filtration, leave the heater running. Restore power and uncover within 60 minutes. Do not exceed that. Extended shutdown starves the biofilter and does its own damage. Note the shutoff time out loud to the customer and to yourself.

ReptilesTier 2 - Moderate

Primary route: Deposition into open enclosures, and contaminated feeder insects

Reptiles are less inhalation-sensitive than birds and less permeable than amphibians, but nearly every enclosure has a screen top. Anything airborne settles straight into the substrate, water dish, and basking surface. Basking animals sit directly under that opening.

The larger practical risk is the feeder colony. Crickets, dubia roaches, mealworms, and superworms are insects. Any residue or drift that reaches their bin either kills the colony or, worse, produces a sublethal feeder that gets handed to the animal as a direct oral dose. Locate the feeder bins during inspection and treat that area as a no-treat zone. Do not apply inside cabinets or closets where feeders are stored.

Handling: cover the enclosure with plastic sheeting, leave heat and UVB on unless the cover creates a fire or overheating risk, uncover after 2 hours. Verify with the customer whether escaped dubias are the reason you were called before you treat for roaches.

03Pre-Service

  • Inquire at time of service. Ask directly: any birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish tanks, or ponds. Customers do not volunteer a 10 gallon tank in a back bedroom.
  • Flag the account permanently in FieldRoutes with the animal type and location, and put the protocol summary in the service notes. The flag exists so a route-cover tech does not walk in and aerosol a wasp nest in the garage.
  • Confirm relocation or containment before treating. For birds and amphibians, confirm the animal has been moved to an untreated room with the door closed. If the customer will not move the animal, do not perform interior service in that area. Convert to exterior-only and document it.
  • Walk the full structure before mixing. Identify every enclosure, tank, pond, feeder bin, and the HVAC return locations.
  • Shut down HVAC before the first application and close supply vents in treated rooms. Limit total HVAC-off time to 60 minutes where tropical reptiles or amphibians remain in the structure, and confirm ambient temperature is holding.

04Baseboard and Interior Liquid Applications

Baseboard treatment is permitted in these homes. The condition is that the animal is out of the room during application and stays out until the product has fully dried. The goal is to put product where the pest is without putting anything in the air, so technique and pressure matter more here than in a standard interior.

  • Animal out of the room first. Enclosure, cage, or tank relocated or covered, door closed, before the first trigger pull.
  • Crack and crevice, spot, and baseboard band. Keep the band tight to the wall and floor junction. No fan pattern indoors.
  • Encapsulated formulations preferred (Suspend PolyZone) where a liquid is warranted. Microencapsulated and polymer-encapsulated products have very low volatility once cured, so the exposure window closes when the surface dries. Wet residue is still the hazard.
  • Minimum 10 ft standoff from any tank, enclosure, cage location, or feeder bin. Do not treat the wall behind or beside a tank, the floor under a stand, or the interior of an aquarium cabinet. Sumps, food, and equipment live in there.
  • No application where runoff or wicking can reach water. Wipe any overspray immediately with a clean damp cloth, not the customer's towel.
  • Full cure before re-entry. Dry to the touch is the label minimum; our standard is longer. See Section 07.
Substitution first
Before you reach for a liquid indoors in one of these homes, ask whether a gel bait, granular bait, bait station, monitor placement, or exclusion repair solves it. In most residential interior callbacks it does, with zero airborne exposure.

05Interior Dusting

Dust is the most underestimated risk in this protocol. Droplets fall out of the air in minutes. Dust does not. Fine particulate stays suspended and continues settling for hours after the tech has left, which means it can land in an open tank or a screen top long after the customer has uncovered everything and gone back to normal.

  • Sealed void injection only. Wall voids, plumbing penetrations, behind escutcheons, under and behind cabinet kick plates, weep holes. No surface dusting, no puffing into open rooms, no dusting along baseboard edges.
  • Do not dust in the same room as any enclosure, tank, or cage location. There is no standoff distance that makes this acceptable. Different room, or not at all.
  • Bellows duster with extension tip, short controlled bursts. Overloading a void is what causes blowback out of the injection point, and that blowback is what gets airborne.
  • Seal the injection point after treatment. Replace the switch plate or escutcheon, or plug with foam or caulk.
  • Formulation choice matters here. Prefer a heavier, moisture-resistant deltamethrin dust over silica gel or diatomaceous products in these homes. Silica-based dusts are extremely light, drift readily, and stay airborne the longest. Save them for structures without sensitive animals.
  • Attic dusting is prohibited in bird households where the attic communicates with an HVAC return or has open can lights, chases, or bath fan penetrations into living space.

06Aerosol Usage

Aerosols combine the two things these animals cannot tolerate: fine airborne droplets and a solvent or propellant carrier. The carrier alone is a respiratory irritant to birds even where the active ingredient would not be.

HouseholdAerosol allowed?Conditions
Bird in structure No Prohibited entirely while the bird is anywhere in the building, including a closed room. If the bird has been fully relocated off-site, crack and crevice straw application is permitted under the conditions at right for reptile households, with full ventilation before return.
Amphibian in structure No Prohibited while the animal is in the building. Same relocation condition as birds.
Aquarium or pond Limited Straw applicator into the void or crack only. Tank covered, air pump and skimmer off, minimum 15 ft standoff, different room from the tank, HVAC off. No trigger release into open air at any distance.
Reptile Limited Straw applicator only, enclosure covered, minimum 15 ft standoff, different room from the enclosure and from any feeder bin, HVAC off.

Preferred substitutions

  • IGR point sources instead of IGR aerosol. A point source is a passive dispenser, so there is no application event and no airborne phase. Juvenile hormone analogs are insect-specific with very low vertebrate toxicity.
  • Gel bait instead of contact aerosol for roaches and ants. Placement is precise, the material is non-volatile, and it is out of reach in a crack.
  • Void foam instead of aerosol where you need to fill a cavity. Foam expands into the void instead of atomizing into the room.
  • Vacuum and mechanical removal for visible populations, spiders, webbing, and cluster flies. Vacuum first is faster than an aerosol knockdown and produces nothing airborne.

Ventilate before the animal comes back. Open windows or run exhaust fans for the final hour. Do not restart the HVAC as your ventilation method while residue is still curing.

08Documentation

  • Account flagged in FieldRoutes with animal type, quantity, and room location.
  • Protocol summary in the recurring service note so it appears for every route-cover tech.
  • Service ticket records: products applied, application method, rooms treated, rooms excluded, HVAC off and on times, tank or enclosure cover and uncover times.
  • Any exposure incident reported to the GM and Technical Manager the same day, with the ticket, the label, and the SDS pulled.
If the customer will not relocate or cover
  1. Do not perform interior service. This is not a negotiation and it is not a customer-satisfaction call.
  2. Convert to exterior-only for that visit and document the refusal on the ticket.
