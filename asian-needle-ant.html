Asian Needle Ant
Control invasive Asian needle ant populations through direct treatment of shallow harborage, thorough perimeter and granular application, and aggressive harborage reduction. This is a medically significant stinging species and a tracked invasive - confirmed identifications must be reported to the state department of agriculture.
PEAK APR - JUL

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing the ants and whether anyone has been stung. Ask specifically about mulch beds, landscape timbers, stacked stone, firewood, and shaded moist areas of the yard. Ask whether anyone in the household has a known insect sting allergy or carries an epinephrine auto-injector.

If anyone reports a sting reaction, direct them to seek medical attention immediately. Asian needle ant stings can cause severe allergic reactions including anaphylaxis in sensitive individuals. Do not assess a sting, do not minimize it, and do not offer medical guidance. Referring them to a physician is always the correct answer.
2
Identification and Biology

Asian needle ant is an invasive species of growing concern in the Southeast. Correct identification matters because it changes both the treatment approach and your reporting obligation.

Appearance

Roughly 1/5 inch, dark brown to black with a subtle sheen. Orange-brown mandibles and antennae tips are the key identifier and are visible with a hand lens. Single node between thorax and abdomen. Possesses a functional stinger.

Key Behavior

Slow, clumsy movers that do not form obvious foraging trails like most pest ants. Often appear to wander individually. Primarily predatory, feeding heavily on termites. Nests are diffuse and shallow in moist, shaded harborage.

Medically Significant Species. Unlike most nuisance ants, Asian needle ant delivers a painful sting and is a documented cause of anaphylaxis in sensitized individuals. Treat customer sting reports seriously and always recommend medical evaluation.
No Trails Means a Different Inspection: Because they do not trail, you cannot follow a line back to the nest the way you would with other ants. Locating this species depends on physically checking harborage - turning stones, pulling back mulch, and lifting timbers.
Why This Species Matters

Asian needle ant displaces native ant species, which disrupts the seed dispersal those natives provide and alters the local ecosystem.

Peak activity and sting incidents occur from spring through mid summer. Colonies may relocate readily, which complicates control.

Because it is invasive and spreading, state agencies are actively tracking its distribution - see the reporting step below.

3
Reporting to the State

Asian needle ant is a tracked invasive species. Confirmed identifications should be reported to the state department of agriculture in the state where the property is located. State agencies use these reports to map distribution and spread.

Reporting Procedure

Collect a specimen where possible. Place several ants in a small vial or sealed bag with rubbing alcohol. Do not crush the specimen - identification depends on intact mandibles and antennae.

Photograph the specimen and the harborage site. Record the property address, date of collection, and the harborage type where they were found.

Notify your Service Manager or GM before reporting so the branch is aware and the submission is handled consistently.

Submit the report to the state department of agriculture plant industry or entomology division for the state the property is in. Requirements vary by state, so confirm the current process with your GM.

Document the identification, the specimen collection, and the report submission in FieldRoutes.

Do Not Skip This Step. Reporting costs a few minutes and contributes directly to tracking an invasive species that poses a genuine public health risk. It also positions ClearDefense as a credible technical partner with state agencies. If you are uncertain about the identification, collect the specimen anyway and escalate to your Technical Manager before reporting.
4
Inspection

This is a harborage-driven inspection. Because the ants do not trail, you must physically check moist, shaded harborage rather than follow foraging activity.

Exterior Priority Harborage

Ground debris: Turn over stones, stepping stones, landscape timbers, railroad ties, bricks, and stacked block. Nests are shallow and diffuse - often just beneath the object.

Mulch and leaf litter: Pull back mulch in beds, particularly on the shaded and damp sides of the property. Check leaf litter accumulations along fence lines and bed edges.

Wood: Firewood piles, fallen logs, rotting stumps, wood-to-soil contact, and any decaying wood on the property. Their preference for termites means decaying wood is prime territory.

Structure adjacent: Foundation cracks, expansion joints, under slabs and porches, potted plants and their saucers, and irrigation and utility boxes.

Moisture zones: Shaded areas, poor drainage points, downspout discharge, over-irrigated beds, and areas that stay damp.

Interior

Interior activity is uncommon but does occur. Check around plumbing penetrations, along baseboards on the affected side, in crawlspaces along sill plates, and in any damp area.

Note that indoor Asian needle ants are typically foragers coming in from exterior harborage rather than an established interior colony.

Wear gloves throughout the inspection. You will be turning stones, lifting timbers, and pulling back mulch by hand. This is where technicians get stung. Never reach into harborage without gloves and a look first.
5
Treatment

Control depends on direct treatment of identified harborage combined with a thorough perimeter barrier. Because colonies are diffuse and relocate readily, thoroughness across all harborage matters more than a single heavy application in one spot.

💧 Direct Harborage Treatment

Where a nest is located under a stone, timber, or debris, treat the site directly with Temprid FX before replacing or removing the object. Pin stream into the soil surface and surrounding area.

Treat under and around all stacked stone, landscape timbers, firewood, and wood-to-soil contact points identified during inspection.

Temprid FX (Beta-Cyfluthrin, Imidacloprid) - Envu
💧 Perimeter Power-Spray

Treat the foundation 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground. Treat around all doors, windows, weep holes, expansion joints, utility and plumbing penetrations, and crawlspace vents.

Fan spray landscape beds heavily so product reaches below the mulch surface where the ants are nesting. Treat a minimum of 4 ft out and further where beds are deep or heavily mulched.

Temprid FX (Beta-Cyfluthrin, Imidacloprid) - Envu
🌾 Granular Application

Apply granule around the structure walking 7 to 9 ft from the foundation, rotating the torso as you turn the handle. Avoid cement walkways, driveways, patios, and decks.

Focus heavily on mulch beds, groundcover, leaf litter zones, and shaded damp areas. Granule carries down into the mulch and litter layer where liquid alone does not fully penetrate.

Wisdom EZ or Wisdom G L/N (Bifenthrin) - per seasonal rotation
🌪 Crack and Crevice Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust into foundation cracks, expansion joints, weep holes, voids in stacked stone and retaining walls, and around utility penetrations where ants are entering or harboring.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
🎯 Bait Consideration

Niban may be applied as a broadcast granular bait in harborage areas and around the perimeter as a supplement to the liquid and granular program.

Set expectations accordingly. Asian needle ants are primarily predatory and feed heavily on termites, so they are less reliably drawn to conventional sweet and protein baits than typical pest ants. Bait supports the program but should not be relied on as the primary control method.

Niban Bait (Orthoboric Acid) - Nisus
6
Customer Recommendations - Harborage and Safety

Harborage removal is the most durable control lever available, and the safety guidance genuinely reduces sting risk for the family.

RecommendationWhy It Matters
Wear gloves in the yard and gardenMost stings occur when a hand contacts an ant while moving stones, mulch, timbers, or firewood. Gloves eliminate the majority of the risk.
Remove stones, timbers, and debrisShallow harborage under ground objects is where colonies establish. Removing it removes the nesting site directly.
Thin mulch to 2 inches or lessDeep mulch holds the moisture these ants require and provides ideal diffuse nesting habitat.
Clear leaf litterLeaf accumulation along beds and fence lines is prime harborage and supports the prey they feed on.
Move firewood away and elevate itFirewood in contact with soil is both harborage and a termite source, which is their preferred food.
Remove rotting wood and stumpsDecaying wood harbors termites, which draws Asian needle ants directly onto the property.
Correct drainage and irrigationReducing standing moisture and over-watered beds makes the property substantially less suitable.
Supervise children in mulched play areasMulched playgrounds and shaded beds are common sting locations for children.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are Asian needle ants - they're an invasive species and they're one of the few ants around here that can actually give you a bad sting, so I want you to take them seriously. If anybody gets stung and has any kind of reaction beyond normal pain, get to a doctor. They nest under stones, timbers, and down in the mulch, and they don't trail like regular ants so you won't see a line of them. I treated all the harborage I found and put a barrier down. Getting that mulch thinned out and those stones and timbers cleared is what really takes the habitat away. I'm also going to report this to the state - they're tracking where this species is spreading."

7
Post Service

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting the identification, harborage locations found, all areas treated, specimen collection, and the state report submission.

Knock on the door and report to the customer, covering the sting risk, the harborage recommendations, and the fact that this is a tracked invasive species. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations and Follow-Up

Advise the customer that Asian needle ant colonies are diffuse and relocate readily, so control typically requires more than one visit and continued harborage reduction on their part.

Activity peaks from spring through mid summer. Expect heavier pressure and more sting reports during that window.

Schedule a follow-up to reassess harborage, retreat nesting sites, and confirm the population is declining. Document activity levels each visit so progress can be tracked accurately.

Escalate Uncertain Identifications: If you are not confident in the identification, collect a specimen in alcohol and contact your Technical Manager before reporting to the state. An accurate report is far more valuable than a fast one.
