General Pest Protocol
Orb Weaving Spiders
Reduce orb weaver webbing through thorough mechanical de-webbing and egg sac removal, targeted treatment of eaves, soffits, and light fixture zones, and reduction of the exterior lighting and flying insect prey that draw them to the structure. Orb weavers are beneficial, non-medically-significant spiders - the nuisance is the webbing.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where the webs are showing up and how quickly they return after being knocked down. Ask specifically about porches, entryways, eaves, exterior light fixtures, windows, decks, and any area the family walks through. Ask whether outdoor lights are left on overnight. Ask whether anyone in the household is fearful of spiders, since that shapes how you set expectations.

2
Identification and Biology

Orb weavers are the classic web-in-the-doorway spider. They are beneficial predators and are not a medical concern, but the webs are a genuine nuisance and the customer's frustration is legitimate.

Appearance

Highly variable in size and color depending on species. Typically a large rounded abdomen, often patterned in brown, gray, yellow, or orange, with legs held in an X pattern at the center of the web. Females are substantially larger than males and are the ones customers notice.

Key Behavior

Builds the classic flat, circular, spoked web in open air. Strictly a web-sitting predator - does not wander, does not hunt across surfaces, and does not enter structures to any meaningful degree. Populations peak in late summer and early fall when females reach full size.

Orb Weavers Are Not a Medical Concern: They are non-aggressive, reluctant to bite, and their venom is not medically significant to people or pets. Reassuring a fearful customer on this point is often the most valuable part of the visit.
The Real Problem Is Light and Prey. Orb weavers build where flying insects are concentrated, which means exterior lighting drives nearly every orb weaver complaint. A spider knocked down tonight will rebuild in the same spot tomorrow if the insects are still there. Surface residual has limited effect on a spider whose feet only touch its own silk.
3
Inspection

Inspect the full exterior with a flashlight, working top to bottom. Orb weavers build in open air spans, so look at the gaps between surfaces rather than only at the surfaces themselves.

Priority Web Locations

Around exterior lighting: Porch lights, garage coach lights, floodlights, landscape lighting, and any fixture left on overnight. This is where the heaviest webbing will be.

Entryways and openings: Front and back door frames, porch corners and ceilings, columns, and the open span across doorways.

Eaves and rooflines: Soffit corners, fascia, gable ends, and the junction where the roofline meets the wall.

Windows: Corners of frames, shutters, and window wells - particularly windows that emit light at night.

Decks and structures: Railings, under decking, pergolas, playsets, patio furniture, and the gaps between spindles.

Other: Around mailboxes, downspouts, hose reels, and the spans between shrubs and the structure.

Prey and Light Assessment

Note which fixtures are left on overnight and what type of bulb is in use. Check for accumulated insects in and around light fixtures.

Check for standing water, dense vegetation, and any moisture source producing the flying insects that draw orb weavers to the structure.

4
De-Webbing - The Primary Control Step

Mechanical removal is the single most impactful thing done on this service. Orb weavers rebuild their web nightly, so removing the web, the spider, and any egg sacs delivers the visible result the customer is paying for.

🧹 De-Webbing Procedure

Dust the de-web head with D-Fense Dust prior to de-webbing (do this in a grassy area and be careful of drift), then de-web the full structure using the Cotton Candy Method.

De-web all corners, doors, windows, soffits, below siding, downspouts, above and below decks, around lights, and around the mailbox. Lightly brush while spinning the de-web head along the entirety of the structure soffits.

Use an extension pole to reach upper soffits, gable ends, and second-story eaves. Orb weavers build high and a ground-level-only pass will leave the worst webbing in place.

Physically remove and dispose of egg sacs - papery tan or brown sacs tucked into protected corners near the web. Each sac contains a large number of spiderlings.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Be Thorough Here: The customer judges this service almost entirely on whether the webs are gone when they walk out the door. Time spent de-webbing produces more perceived value than any other part of the visit.
5
Chemical Treatment

Liquid application treats spiders contacting the structure and, just as importantly, reduces the flying insect prey that draws orb weavers in. Dust handles the protected voids where they retreat and where egg sacs are placed.

💧 Power-Spray - Upper Structure and Web Zones

Treat around and behind all exterior light fixtures, eaves, soffits, fascia, gable ends, and rooflines. This is where the service is won on an orb weaver account.

Treat around all door and window frames, porch ceilings and corners, columns, deck railings and underside, pergolas, and around the mailbox and downspouts.

Use an extension pole or adjust the spray pattern to reach upper trim and soffit lines thoroughly.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envuor Bifen IT (Bifenthrin) - CSI
💧 Foundation and Perimeter

Treat the foundation 3 ft up the exterior wall and 4 ft out on the ground. Treat landscape beds and shrubs adjacent to the structure where webs span from vegetation to wall.

Suspend PolyZone (Deltamethrin) - Envuor Bifen IT (Bifenthrin) - CSI
🌪 Crack and Crevice Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust into soffit and fascia gaps, behind shutters, into porch ceiling corners and column voids, around light fixture housings, and into gaps below siding where spiders retreat during the day and place egg sacs.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Set Realistic Chemical Expectations: An orb weaver sits on silk, not on the treated surface, so residual contact is limited compared to a ground-hunting spider. Explain to the customer that the de-webbing plus reducing the insects and lighting is what actually holds - the spray supports it rather than carrying it alone.
6
Customer Recommendations - Lighting and Prey Reduction

Orb weaver pressure is driven almost entirely by the flying insects attracted to the home at night. These changes address the actual cause and are what keep webs from returning within days.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Switch to yellow or sodium vapor bulbsStandard white bulbs attract far more flying insects. Yellow bug bulbs or warm LED dramatically reduce the prey supply, which is the single most effective change available.
Turn off unnecessary exterior lightsLights left on overnight are the primary driver of orb weaver activity. Using lights only when needed removes the insect concentration.
Relocate fixtures away from doorsMoving light away from entryways, or using motion-activated fixtures, keeps the insect cloud and the webs away from the doors the family uses.
Close blinds and curtains at nightInterior light spilling through windows draws insects to the glass and produces webbing in window corners.
Knock down new webs promptlyA broom or hose on new webbing between services discourages rebuilding in high-traffic spots like doorways.
Trim vegetation off the structureRemoving shrub and tree contact eliminates the anchor points orb weavers use to span webs to the wall.
Address standing waterEliminating standing water reduces the mosquitoes and midges that feed the spider population.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"Orb weavers are the ones that build that classic round web across your doorway overnight. They're completely harmless - they're not going to come after anybody and they don't come inside. Here's the thing though: they build where the bugs are, and the bugs are there because of your porch lights. I de-webbed the whole house and treated up around the soffits and fixtures. If you swap those porch bulbs to yellow ones, that'll cut down the insects and you'll see way fewer webs going back up."

7
Post Service

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting where webbing was heaviest, how many egg sacs were removed, lighting conditions observed, and all areas de-webbed and treated.

Knock on the door and report to the customer, walking them around to show the de-webbing results and covering the lighting recommendations. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations and Follow-Up

Advise the customer that some webbing will return, particularly around lights that stay on overnight. This is normal and does not indicate a treatment failure - orb weavers rebuild nightly and new spiders move in from surrounding vegetation.

Orb weaver populations peak in late summer and early fall when females reach full size and webs become most noticeable. Expect heavier pressure and more frequent de-webbing needs during this window.

Regular de-webbing on the quarterly route is what keeps these accounts satisfied. Where pressure is heavy, discuss more frequent service during the peak season.
