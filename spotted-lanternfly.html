General Pest Protocol
Spotted Lanternfly
Reduce spotted lanternfly populations through egg mass destruction, host tree identification and management, and targeted contact treatment. This is a regulated invasive agricultural pest - confirmed sightings must be reported to the state department of agriculture.
PEAK JUL - OCT
Field Identification
Spotted Lanternfly
Lycorma delicatula
Adult: ~1 in at rest. Gray forewings with black spots; hindwings show a striking red patch with black and white bands when open. Late nymph: Bright red with black and white markings. Early nymph: Black with white spots. Egg mass: 1 to 1.5 in gray-brown patch resembling dried mud. Host: Tree of Heaven.

Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing the insects and on which trees or surfaces. Ask whether they have noticed sticky residue or black sooty coating on decks, patio furniture, vehicles, or plants underneath trees. Ask whether they have Tree of Heaven on the property, and whether they have recently received deliveries, firewood, nursery stock, or vehicles from out of state.

2
Identification and Life Stages

Spotted lanternfly is an invasive planthopper and a serious agricultural pest. Correct identification by life stage matters because the appearance changes dramatically through the season and each stage is handled differently.

Life StageAppearanceTypical Window
Egg Mass1 to 1.5 inch gray-brown patch resembling dried mud or a smear of putty. Ages to a cracked, scaly appearance. Contains 30 to 50 eggs.Sep through Jun
Early NymphBlack with bright white spots, wingless, roughly 1/4 inch. Jumps readily when disturbed.Apr through Jul
Late NymphBright red with black markings and white spots, roughly 1/2 inch. Highly distinctive and hard to mistake.Jul through Sep
AdultAbout 1 inch at rest. Gray forewings with black spots; hindwings show a striking red patch with black and white bands when open. Abdomen yellow with black bands.Jul through Dec
Key Behavior and Damage

Feeds by piercing plant tissue and drawing sap. Does not bite or sting people or pets and does not damage the structure or its contents.

Excretes large volumes of honeydew, which coats surfaces below and promotes growth of black sooty mold on decks, furniture, vehicles, and vegetation. The honeydew also attracts wasps, hornets, ants, and flies - which is frequently the actual complaint that generates the call.

Tree of Heaven (Ailanthus altissima) is the strongly preferred host. Where it is present on or near a property, it is driving the infestation. Also feeds on grape, maple, walnut, willow, birch, and many ornamentals.

Egg Masses Travel. Spotted lanternfly spreads primarily by egg masses laid on vehicles, trailers, firewood, outdoor furniture, nursery stock, and stone. This is why quarantine and inspection matter and why removal of egg masses is such a high-value action.
3
Reporting to the State

Spotted lanternfly is a regulated invasive pest and confirmed sightings must be reported to the state department of agriculture in the state where the property is located. States maintain quarantine zones and actively track the leading edge of spread, and reports from the field directly inform that mapping.

Reporting Procedure

Collect a specimen where possible. Place the insect in a sealed bag or vial with rubbing alcohol. If you find an egg mass, photograph it in place before scraping.

Photograph the specimen, the host plant or surface, and the surrounding area. Record the property address, date, life stage observed, and approximate numbers.

Notify your Service Manager or GM before reporting so the branch is aware and submissions are handled consistently.

Submit through the state department of agriculture plant industry or plant certification division. Many states operate a dedicated spotted lanternfly reporting portal or hotline. Confirm the current process for your state with your GM.

Document the sighting, life stage, specimen collection, and report submission in FieldRoutes.

Report Every Confirmed Sighting. This is not optional courtesy - it is how quarantine boundaries get drawn and how agricultural producers get advance warning. Reports from a property outside a known quarantine zone are especially valuable. If you are uncertain about the identification, collect a specimen and escalate to your Technical Manager before reporting.
Quarantine Awareness: Where a property falls inside a state quarantine zone, movement of firewood, nursery stock, landscaping material, and outdoor items may be regulated. Advise the customer to check their state requirements before moving material off the property, and inspect your own vehicle and equipment before leaving.
4
Inspection

Inspect the property systematically. What you are looking for depends on the season, so orient your inspection to the life stage that should be present.

Host Tree Assessment

Identify Tree of Heaven first. Compound leaves with 10 to 40 leaflets, smooth gray bark, and a strong unpleasant odor when a leaf or twig is crushed. If present, it is the center of the infestation and everything else follows from it.

Note other host trees on the property: grape vines, maple, walnut, willow, birch, and ornamental plantings.

Look for weeping wounds, oozing sap, and gray or black staining running down trunks - all indicators of heavy feeding activity above.

Egg Mass Locations - Sep through Jun

Scan tree trunks and the underside of large limbs, starting at eye level and working up and down. Egg masses are frequently placed higher than expected.

Also check smooth vertical surfaces: stone and brick walls, fence posts and rails, outdoor furniture, grills, firewood stacks, landscape stone, play equipment, trailers, RVs, boats, and vehicles left parked.

Check the underside of horizontal surfaces such as deck rails, benches, and equipment where they are protected from weather.

Nymph and Adult Activity - Apr through Dec

Look on trunks and stems of host trees, particularly on the sunny side. Nymphs congregate on new growth and tender stems.

Look for honeydew and sooty mold on surfaces below host trees - this indicates a heavy population feeding overhead even where you cannot see the insects.

Note wasp, hornet, and ant activity around the honeydew, since this is frequently what actually prompted the customer to call.

5
Mechanical Control - Egg Mass Removal

Egg mass destruction is the highest-value action available on this pest and is entirely mechanical. A single mass eliminated is 30 to 50 insects that never hatch, and it directly slows spread.

🧹 Scraping Procedure

Using a plastic scraper, putty knife, or stiff card, scrape the egg mass off the surface into a sealed bag or container holding rubbing alcohol or hand sanitizer.

Scraping alone is not sufficient. Eggs can still hatch if simply knocked to the ground. They must be crushed or fully submerged in alcohol to be destroyed.

Seal the bag and dispose of it in the trash. Do not leave scraped material on the ground.

Work systematically across trunks, walls, furniture, stone, and any surface where masses were found during inspection. Document the count in FieldRoutes.

Teach the Customer to Do This. Egg mass scraping requires no license and no equipment. Showing the customer what an egg mass looks like and how to scrape and destroy it turns them into an ongoing asset between services. This is one of the highest-impact things you can leave behind.
Do not use sticky bands on trees. Sticky bands intended for lanternfly nymphs capture large numbers of non-target animals including birds, bats, squirrels, and beneficial insects. Where a customer has installed them, advise removal and recommend a properly screened circle trap instead.
6
Chemical Treatment

Chemical work supplements host tree management and mechanical control. Match the application to the life stage present and always confirm the target site is on the product label.

💧 Contact Treatment - Nymphs and Adults

Where nymphs or adults are congregating on trunks, stems, or hard surfaces, apply a direct contact treatment with Precor Outdoor F-T-M. Target the insects directly and the surfaces they are resting on.

Treat the base and lower trunk of heavily infested host trees, structural surfaces where adults congregate, and fence lines and hardscape adjacent to host trees.

Confirm the current mix rate and approved target sites on the product label before mixing. Ornamental and outdoor surface sites must be listed on the label for the application being made.

Precor Outdoor F-T-M Concentrate - Zoecon
💧 Structural Perimeter

Where adults are moving onto the home in large numbers in late season, treat the foundation 3 ft up and 4 ft out, around doors and windows, and on sun-facing walls where they congregate.

This is a nuisance-reduction measure. It does not address the population feeding in the trees.

Precor Outdoor F-T-M Concentrate - Zoecon
Pollinator Protection: Avoid drift onto flowering plants and blooming vegetation. Do not treat blooms. Be mindful of wind conditions, and never apply to food crops, grape vines intended for harvest, or vegetable gardens unless the label specifically permits that site.
Scope Limits: ClearDefense does not perform systemic trunk injection, basal bark treatment, or commercial agricultural applications for spotted lanternfly. Where a property has significant host tree infestation requiring systemic treatment, or where vineyards, orchards, or nursery stock are involved, refer the customer to a licensed arborist or an agricultural applicator. Escalate to your GM before committing to any tree treatment scope.
7
Customer Recommendations - Host Tree and Property Management

Long-term reduction depends on removing the preferred host and denying egg laying sites. Walk the customer through these on every spotted lanternfly service.

RecommendationWhy It Matters
Remove Tree of HeavenThe strongly preferred host. Removing it is the single most effective long-term action. Refer to a licensed arborist - it resprouts aggressively and requires proper herbicide treatment of the stump to prevent regrowth.
Scrape and destroy egg massesEach mass destroyed is 30 to 50 insects that never hatch. Customers can do this year round from September through June with no special equipment.
Inspect before moving anythingCheck vehicles, trailers, firewood, outdoor furniture, and nursery stock for egg masses before moving them off the property. This is the primary spread mechanism.
Do not move firewoodBuy and burn firewood locally. Moving firewood transports egg masses across county and state lines.
Wash honeydew off surfacesCleaning decks, furniture, and vehicles promptly prevents sooty mold from setting and reduces the wasp and ant activity the honeydew attracts.
Remove sticky bands if presentSticky bands kill birds, bats, and beneficial wildlife. Replace with a properly screened circle trap if trapping is desired.
Report sightings themselvesEncourage the customer to report to their state department of agriculture as well. More data points produce better tracking.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"This is spotted lanternfly - it's an invasive species and the state is actively tracking where it's showing up, so I'm going to report this sighting. Good news is they don't bite and they won't hurt your house. The sticky mess on your deck is honeydew from them feeding up in the tree, and that's what's drawing all the wasps. The biggest thing here is that Tree of Heaven in the back corner - that's their favorite host and it's basically feeding the whole population. Getting an arborist to take that out would make a real difference. In the meantime, let me show you what the egg masses look like, because scraping those off and dropping them in a bag with alcohol is something you can do all winter and it genuinely helps."

8
Post Service

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting the life stage observed, approximate numbers, host trees identified, egg mass count removed, areas treated, and the state report submission.

Knock on the door and report to the customer, covering the host tree recommendation, egg mass scraping technique, and the reporting you submitted. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Inspect your vehicle and equipment before leaving the property to avoid transporting egg masses or adults to the next account.

Setting Expectations and Follow-Up

Advise the customer that spotted lanternfly are strong movers and adults will continue to fly in from surrounding properties and wooded areas. Treatment reduces the population on their property but cannot prevent reinfestation from off site.

Populations peak from late summer into fall as adults mature, which is when honeydew, sooty mold, and wasp activity are worst. Expect the heaviest complaints during that window.

Schedule follow-up service through the active season, and a fall or winter visit specifically for egg mass inspection and removal.
