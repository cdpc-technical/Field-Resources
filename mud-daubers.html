General Pest Protocol
Mud Daubers
Remove mud dauber nests mechanically and apply residual treatment to the surfaces they build on, while reducing the spider population that draws them to the structure. Mud daubers are solitary, non-territorial and extremely reluctant to sting - this is a nuisance and cosmetic pest.
PEAK APR - SEP
Field Identification
Mud Dauber
Sceliphron / Trypoxylon spp.
Size: ~1 in, slender with a very long thread-like waist. Color: Glossy black, black with blue sheen, or black and yellow depending on species. Nest: Hardened mud - either a smooth lump of packed cells or a set of vertical organ pipe tubes on sheltered walls, eaves and ceilings. Behavior: Solitary, non-territorial, extremely docile. Preys on spiders to provision its cells.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing the mud nests and how long they have been there. Ask specifically about eaves, soffits, porch and patio ceilings, garages, sheds, and under decks. Ask whether they have had a spider problem, since mud daubers hunt spiders and heavy spider activity draws them in.

Reiterate that stinging insects are warrantied and they should not hesitate to contact us.

2
Identification and Biology

Mud daubers are among the most harmless stinging insects we deal with. Most of this call is reassurance plus mechanical removal.

The Wasp

Roughly 1 in, slender, with a distinctive very long thread-like waist. Glossy black, black with a metallic blue sheen, or black and yellow depending on species. Flies slowly and deliberately.

The Nest

Hardened mud. Either a smooth lump of packed cells, or a cluster of vertical organ pipe tubes roughly finger-thick. Built on sheltered vertical walls and ceilings protected from rain.

Solitary and Docile: Mud daubers are solitary - one female per nest, no colony, no workers. They do not defend the nest, are extremely reluctant to sting, and stings are rare even when the nest is being scraped off. This is genuinely a nuisance and cosmetic pest, not a safety threat.
Why They Are There

Mud daubers hunt spiders, paralyze them, and pack them into the mud cells as food for their larvae. A heavy mud dauber presence is usually a sign of a healthy spider population on the structure.

The female builds the nest, provisions each cell with spiders, lays an egg, and seals it. Larvae develop inside and emerge the following season.

Active spring through late summer, with nests visible year round since the mud persists long after the wasp is gone.

Check Before You Scrape: Old abandoned mud dauber nests are frequently taken over by other wasps, and can also be used by black widows as protected harborage. Inspect a nest before removing it by hand.
3
Inspection

Inspect the full exterior and all outbuildings with a flashlight, working top to bottom. Mud daubers build in sheltered spots protected from rain.

Priority Nest Locations

Under eaves, soffits and overhangs - the most common location by far.

Porch and patio ceilings, under covered entryways, and beneath second-story decks.

Garages, sheds, barns and carports - especially on rafters, ceiling joists, and interior walls. Open garages are prime.

Behind shutters, on siding under protected overhangs, inside electrical and meter boxes, and on stored equipment.

Attics and crawlspaces where access is available to them.

Assess Spider Pressure

Note webbing, spider activity, and de-webbing needs at the same time. Reducing the spider population directly reduces mud dauber pressure, since spiders are the entire reason they are provisioning nests there.

4
Control - Mechanical Removal

Physical removal is the primary control and is what the customer actually sees. The nests are cosmetic damage as much as anything, so thorough removal is the deliverable.

🧹 Nest Removal and De-Webbing

Dust the de-web head with D-Fense Dust prior to de-webbing (do this in a grassy area and be careful of drift), then de-web the full structure using the Cotton Candy Method.

Scrape all mud nests from eaves, soffits, ceilings, walls and outbuildings. Use the de-webber pole or a scraper for elevated nests.

Remove nests completely - a partial scrape leaves a rough mud base that the wasp will simply rebuild on.

Dispose of removed nests rather than leaving them on the ground.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Cleaning the Surface

Mud nests leave a stain or residue on siding and painted surfaces. Advise the customer that the mark may need washing after removal.

Recommend they scrape any new nests promptly rather than letting them harden and accumulate through the season.

5
Control - Chemical Treatment

Residual treatment discourages rebuilding on the cleared surfaces and reduces the spiders that draw them in.

💧 Power-Spray - Nest Zones

Following removal, treat the surfaces where nests were built - eaves, soffits, fascia, porch and patio ceilings, and the upper wall areas beneath overhangs.

Extend treatment to garage and outbuilding rafters, ceiling joists and interior walls where nests were found.

Use an extension pole where needed. These wasps build high, so a ground-level application will not protect the zones that matter.

Route Mix
💧 Foundation and Perimeter

Treat the foundation 3 ft up and 4 ft out, and around all doors, windows and entry points as part of the standard exterior service.

Route or Start Mix
🕷 Spider Reduction - The Real Lever

Treat for spiders as part of the same service. Thorough de-webbing plus residual on the eaves, soffits and light fixture zones reduces the spider population directly.

Fewer spiders means fewer mud daubers. Where a customer has recurring mud dauber pressure, the spider population is the underlying driver and should be addressed as the primary target.

Pollinator Protection: Avoid drift onto flowering plants and blooming vegetation. Be mindful of wind conditions.
6
Customer Recommendations

Mud dauber prevention is mostly about reducing spiders and staying on top of new nests before they harden and accumulate.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Knock down new nests earlyA fresh mud nest scrapes off easily. Once hardened and stacked over a season it stains the surface and takes real effort to remove.
Reduce exterior lighting or switch bulbsLights draw insects, insects draw spiders, and spiders draw mud daubers. Yellow bulbs break the chain at the source.
Keep up with de-webbingFewer spiders on the structure means far less reason for mud daubers to build there.
Close up sheds and garagesOpen outbuildings are prime nesting sites. Keeping doors closed during the day removes easy access.
Wash stained surfaces after removalMud leaves a mark on siding and paint. Washing promptly after scraping prevents a permanent stain.
Do not fear the waspSolitary, non-territorial, and extremely reluctant to sting. Reassure customers who are avoiding their own porch.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"Mud daubers are about the most harmless wasp out there - they're solitary, there's no colony, and they won't defend the nest. I scraped everything off and treated those areas so they don't rebuild. Here's the part most folks don't know though: they're here hunting spiders. That's what they pack in those mud tubes to feed their young. So the real answer to keeping them away long term is knocking the spider population down, which is what the de-webbing and the treatment on your eaves is doing."

7
Communication and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting nest locations and how many were removed, spider activity observed, areas treated, and all recommendations made.

Report to the customer, walking them around to show the removal results. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all back yard gates are secure and all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations

Advise the customer that some new nest-building may occur through the active season, particularly in sheltered spots and where spider pressure remains high.

Regular de-webbing and nest removal on the quarterly route is what keeps these accounts clear.

Where pressure is heavy, discuss more frequent service during spring and summer, and address the spider population as the primary target.
