General Pest Protocol
Pantry Beetles
Eliminate cigarette and drugstore beetle infestations by locating and discarding the infested stored product, performing thorough sanitation, placing Xlure RTU pheromone traps for capture and monitoring, and applying targeted crack and crevice treatment to emptied surfaces. Source removal is the service - trap counts confirm whether it worked.
YEAR ROUND
Field Identification
Pantry Beetles
Cigarette & Drugstore Beetle
Both: ~1/8 in, reddish-brown, oval and humpbacked - the head tucks under the thorax so it is not visible from above. Both fly readily and are drawn to light. Cigarette Beetle: Saw-toothed (serrated) antennae and smooth wing covers. Drugstore Beetle: antennae end in a 3-segmented club and wing covers carry lengthwise grooves. That antenna and elytra check is the definitive separation.

1
Arrival and Intro

Park in the road if possible, or on the side of the house. Clock in to app in FieldRoutes. Knock and ring the doorbell (Mandatory).

Ask where they are seeing the beetles and whether they have found any in a food product. Ask how long it has been occurring and whether beetles are showing up at windows or light fixtures, which is common. Ask specifically about spices, dry pet food, bird seed, bulk grain and flour, and decorative dried items.

Explain up front that this service depends on locating and discarding the infested product, and that you will need access to the pantry and all food storage areas.

2
Identification and Biology

Cigarette and drugstore beetles are close relatives, look nearly identical at a glance, and are treated the same way. Confirming which one you have still matters, because their preferred food sources differ and that tells you where to search hardest.

Cigarette Beetle

Lasioderma serricorne. Saw-toothed (serrated) antennae and smooth, unmarked wing covers. Slightly more rounded profile.

Favors: Spices - especially paprika, chili powder and curry - plus tobacco, dry pet food, bird seed, cereals, dried flowers and potpourri.

Drugstore Beetle

Stegobium paniceum. Antennae end in a 3-segmented club and the wing covers carry distinct lengthwise grooves.

Favors: An exceptionally broad diet - flour, bread, spices, pet food, and famously non-food items including pharmaceuticals, book bindings, leather and paper.

The Humpback Shape Is Your First Clue: Both species tuck the head down under the thorax, giving a distinctly hunched profile from the side. Combined with the small size and reddish-brown color, that shape separates them from flour beetles and other stored product pests before you even reach for a hand lens.
Biology That Drives the Service

They are strong fliers and are drawn to light. This is why customers first notice them at windows, lamps and ceiling fixtures - often in a different room from the actual infested product.

The complete life cycle runs roughly 6 to 8 weeks in normal household conditions and faster in warm conditions.

Larvae bore into product and can chew through plastic bags, cardboard and foil. Sealed original packaging is not protection.

Adults live only a few weeks and do not feed much - all the damage is done by larvae inside the product.

Drugstore Beetles Do Not Stay in the Pantry: Because they will feed on paper, leather, book bindings and even pharmaceuticals, a drugstore beetle infestation can persist in a bookshelf, a medicine cabinet or a box of old paperwork long after every food item has been discarded. Widen the search on these accounts.
3
Inspection and Source Identification

Finding and removing the infested product is the service. Chemical work supports it but cannot substitute for it. Be thorough - the source is very frequently an item the customer forgot they owned.

Inspect Every Dry Product

Check all dry goods: flour, cornmeal, rice, pasta, cereal, crackers, dried fruit, nuts, seeds, chocolate, baking supplies and powdered drink mix.

Spices are the number one missed source on cigarette beetle jobs. Open and inspect every jar and packet, particularly paprika, chili powder, curry and other ground red spices. Beetles will be inside the container.

Also commonly missed: dry pet food, bird seed, grass seed, decorative dried flowers, Indian corn, potpourri, wreaths, and rodent bait left in stations.

Pour suspect product into a light-colored bowl to check. Look for live adults, larvae, shed skins, fine powder and small round exit holes in packaging.

Widen the Search for Drugstore Beetles

Bookshelves and leather-bound books, stored paperwork and cardboard, dried plant material, medicine cabinets and vitamin bottles, and pet treats.

These sources sustain a population indefinitely and are the usual reason a job appears to fail after the pantry was cleared.

Inspect the Structure

Pantry and cabinet corners, shelf edges, the underside of shelving, cabinet hinges, and the void behind cabinet frames.

Check window sills and light fixtures - collecting dead beetles there confirms activity and helps locate which room the source is in.

Check adjacent rooms. Because they fly, adults disperse well away from the source.

Every infested product must be discarded. Do not attempt to salvage. Bag infested items, seal the bag, and have the customer remove it to the outdoor bin immediately - not to an indoor trash can where adults can emerge and reinfest.
4
Source Removal and Sanitation

Physical removal and cleaning eliminate the population. This step does more than any product applied.

Removal and Cleaning Sequence

Have the customer discard all infested product into sealed bags removed to the exterior bin.

Completely empty the affected pantry and cabinets. Partial removal leaves eggs and larvae behind.

Vacuum thoroughly - shelves, shelf edges, corners, cracks, hinges and the floor beneath. Vacuuming physically removes every life stage and is the single most effective sanitation step. Dispose of the vacuum bag or empty the canister outside immediately.

Wipe shelving with soap and water and allow surfaces to dry fully before restocking.

Advise transferring all retained dry goods into hard-sided airtight containers - glass, metal or heavy plastic. Larvae chew through bags and thin cardboard.

Freezing Salvageable Product

Where the customer wants to keep an unopened item that may have been exposed, advise freezing at 0 degrees F for a minimum of 4 to 7 days to kill all life stages, then storing it in an airtight container.

5
Xlure RTU Pheromone Trap Placement

ClearDefense uses Xlure RTU pheromone traps for stored product pests. The trap attracts and captures adult beetles and, just as importantly, provides the monitoring data that tells you whether the source was actually eliminated.

📌 Placement

Place traps in and near the affected pantry, cabinets and food storage areas. Position at or slightly above the level of the stored product where practical.

Place traps away from strong air currents, HVAC vents, fans and direct sunlight, all of which disrupt the pheromone plume.

Use multiple traps where the infestation spans more than one room. Because these beetles fly, also place traps in adjacent rooms and near windows where adults have been sighted.

Do not place traps directly against open food. Keep them out of reach of children and pets.

Confirm the lure matches the target species before placing - verify on the packaging.

Xlure RTU Pheromone Trap
📈 Monitoring Value

Trap counts are your diagnostic tool. Record the count at every visit.

Declining counts over successive visits confirm the source was eliminated and the population is dying out.

Counts that stay level or rise mean an infested product was missed. Re-inspect thoroughly rather than simply replacing the trap and retreating.

Trap placement also helps triangulate the source - the room with consistently higher counts is where to search hardest.

Replace traps per label interval as the lure depletes. Note replacement dates in FieldRoutes.

Explain the Trap Honestly: Xlure RTU captures adults and tells us whether the problem is resolving, but it will not by itself eliminate an infestation while infested product remains in the home. Set that expectation so the customer does not treat the trap as the whole solution.
6
Chemical Treatment

Chemical application is limited, targeted, and applied only to emptied and cleaned surfaces. It handles larvae and adults hiding in cracks that vacuuming missed.

💧 Crack and Crevice Application

Apply only after the pantry and cabinets have been emptied, vacuumed and cleaned. Never treat with food present.

Treat cracks and crevices in shelving, shelf edges, cabinet corners and seams, hinge voids, and the junction where shelving meets the wall.

Allow surfaces to dry completely before the customer restocks. Advise lining shelves with fresh paper where preferred.

Onslaught FastCap (Multi Active) - MGK
🌪 Void Dusting

Apply D-Fense Dust into inaccessible voids behind cabinet frames, in hinge voids and along wall and shelf junctions where liquid cannot reach.

Apply a light, even coating. Do not apply where dust could contact food or food-contact surfaces.

D-Fense Dust (Deltamethrin) - CSI
Never apply product to food, food-contact surfaces, or areas where food is stored unprotected. All product must be applied to emptied, cleaned surfaces and be fully dry before restocking. Confirm the site is on the product label.
7
Customer Recommendations

Prevention is straightforward and entirely within the customer's control. Cover each point before leaving.

RecommendationWhy It Works
Store dry goods in airtight containersGlass, metal or heavy plastic. Larvae chew straight through bags, paper and thin cardboard, so original packaging is not protection.
Do not overlook the spice rackGround red spices are the single most common cigarette beetle source and the one customers never suspect.
Inspect groceries at purchaseInfestations almost always arrive in a purchased product. Check packaging for damage, powder or small holes.
Buy smaller quantitiesBulk items stored for months are the most common source. Buy what will be used in a reasonable timeframe.
Rotate stock, oldest firstPrevents items sitting forgotten at the back of a pantry long enough to develop an infestation.
Include pet food and bird seedDry pet food, bird seed and grass seed are frequent sources. Store them in sealed containers too.
Check decorative and paper itemsDried flowers, potpourri and Indian corn for both species; books, leather and stored paperwork for drugstore beetles.
Leave the traps in placeTraps tell us whether the problem is resolving. Moving or discarding them removes the monitoring value.
Suggested Customer Talk Track

"These are pantry beetles, and they came in with something you bought - that's almost always how it happens, so don't feel bad. The one that catches everybody out is the spice rack; a jar of paprika sitting in the back for two years is the usual culprit. They also fly and head for the light, which is why you're finding them at the window instead of in the cabinet. I've set pheromone traps - those catch the adults, but more importantly they tell me whether we actually got the source. If those counts don't drop by my next visit, something's still in here and we'll go hunting again."

8
Post Service and Follow-Up

Complete a detailed service report in FieldRoutes documenting which species was identified, the infested product found and discarded, all areas inspected and treated, trap locations and initial counts, and all recommendations made.

Report to the customer, emphasizing that beetles will continue emerging for several weeks and that trap counts are how we confirm success. Get a signature on the iPad and leave a review card.

Ensure all equipment is loaded in your vehicle.

Setting Expectations and Follow-Up

Advise the customer that adults will continue to appear for several weeks after the source is removed as larvae already developing complete their cycle. This is expected and is not a treatment failure.

Because the life cycle runs 6 to 8 weeks, schedule follow-up at 2 to 4 week intervals to check trap counts, replace traps per label and reinspect.

If trap counts are not declining by the second visit, an infested product was missed. Conduct a full re-inspection including spices, pet food, bird seed, decorative items, and for drugstore beetles the bookshelves and stored paper.

Do not declare control until trap counts have dropped to zero and remained there across consecutive visits.
